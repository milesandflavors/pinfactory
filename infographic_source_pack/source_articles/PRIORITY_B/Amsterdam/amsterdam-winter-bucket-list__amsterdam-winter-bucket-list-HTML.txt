<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="new-years-dive">The Nieuwjaarsduik at Sloterplas</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This is the one that sounds like a joke until you're standing on the shore watching people actually run into freezing water on January 1st. The biggest Nieuwjaarsduik in the Netherlands happens at Scheveningen near The Hague, drawing around 10,000 swimmers at noon sharp — but Amsterdam runs its own, smaller version at Sloterplas, without the crowds or the travel out of the city.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You don't need to swim to enjoy it — plenty of people show up purely to watch. If you do want to jump in, most local dives are informal and free to join, with hot soup typically on offer afterward to warm back up.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Price:</strong> Free | <strong>Best time:</strong> January 1st, midday | <strong>Time needed:</strong> 1-2 hours | <strong>Tip:</strong> Bring a towel, dry clothes, and something warm to change into immediately — the actual swim lasts seconds, but the walk back doesn't.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="museumplein-rink">The Museumplein Ice Rink</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This is the pick I'd send a first-timer to over another canal walk. The Museumplein Ice Rink is Amsterdam's largest outdoor rink, set right in the museum quarter with the Rijksmuseum, Van Gogh Museum, and Stedelijk all visible from the ice. It typically runs from mid-November into early February, and admission includes skate rental.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It's freely accessible to walk around even if you don't want to skate, which makes it an easy stop to fold into a museum day rather than a whole separate outing. Online booking is worth doing ahead of weekends and the Christmas holiday stretch specifically, since those days queue up fast.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Price:</strong> Around €16 including skate rental | <strong>Best time:</strong> Weekday mornings, mid-November through early February | <strong>Time needed:</strong> 1-2 hours | <strong>Tip:</strong> Book online ahead of weekends — walk-up queues get long once the museums let out for the day.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="hortus">Hortus Botanicus, Not a Museum Queue</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>When the canals lose their charm around hour three of a cold walk, the Hortus Botanicus is the better move. It's one of the world's oldest botanical gardens, with a tropical greenhouse and a Palm House that turn a January afternoon into something closer to a different climate entirely — genuinely warm, humid air, and considerably fewer crowds than the Rijksmuseum next door.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It's open daily in winter, and the Hortus runs special Winter Evenings with reduced admission after 4:30pm, which pairs well with an earlier museum visit on the same day.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Price:</strong> €12 adult, €8 student/senior, €6 ages 6-12, free under 6; €7 after 4:30pm in winter | <strong>Best time:</strong> Late afternoon for the reduced Winter Evening rate | <strong>Time needed:</strong> 1-1.5 hours | <strong>Tip:</strong> Head straight for the Palm House first — it's the warmest room and the best contrast to the cold outside.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="oliebollen">An Oliebollen Stand, Not a Bakery Case</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Oliebollen — deep-fried, raisin-studded dough balls dusted in powdered sugar — show up in bakery windows from October, but the real tradition is a street stand, not a case. Stalls like Oliebollenkraam JT van Gemert or Bakker's Oliebollenkraam in Westerpark are where locals actually queue, especially as New Year's Eve approaches and lines start forming as early as 8am at the most popular spots.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This is the small, specific, genuinely cozy reason to be outside on a freezing day that has nothing to do with any market — just a paper bag, powdered sugar, and a few minutes standing in the cold that are entirely worth it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Price:</strong> A few euros per bag | <strong>Best time:</strong> Late December, avoid the New Year's Eve morning rush unless you enjoy the queue | <strong>Time needed:</strong> 15-20 minutes | <strong>Tip:</strong> Confirm the current stand's exact location before heading out — the seasonal carts move around slightly year to year.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="quiet-museums">Rijksmuseum and Van Gogh in Their Quietest Season</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>January and February are the calmest months of the year at Amsterdam's major museums — the holiday crowds are gone and the tulip-season tourist wave hasn't started. If a genuinely uncrowded visit to the Rijksmuseum or Van Gogh Museum is the goal, this is the actual window. For the full comparison of which one deserves more of your time, see <a href="/rijksmuseum-vs-van-gogh-museum/">the honest breakdown</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Price:</strong> Varies by museum; check current admission | <strong>Best time:</strong> Weekday mornings in January or February | <strong>Time needed:</strong> Half a day for both | <strong>Tip:</strong> This is also the cheapest hotel season in the city — pair a museum-heavy trip with off-peak room rates.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
