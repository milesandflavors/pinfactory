<p>Every winter, Amsterdam's canals turn into an open-air art gallery for the Amsterdam Light Festival — illuminated installations from artists around the world, strung along the water where you'd normally just see canal houses and bridges. The 2026-27 edition runs November 26, 2026 through January 17, 2027, which means it overlaps almost the entire Christmas and New Year's season.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This guide covers the route, how to see it on foot versus by boat, and when to go.</p>

<h2 id="water-colors-route">The Water Colors Route</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The festival's signature route, Water Colors, follows the canals for roughly 4 to 5 kilometers — about two hours on foot at a comfortable pace, more if you stop to look at each installation. Artists from around the world contribute illuminated works spread along the route, so no two years look quite the same, even if the general path stays consistent.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The route runs through the historic canal belt, which means you're getting the festival's art on top of Amsterdam's own canal-house scenery — genuinely one of the better free things to do in the city during the season, if you don't mind the cold and the walking distance.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 id="walking-vs-boat">Walking It vs. Seeing It by Boat</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Walking the route is free and lets you set your own pace — stop as long as you want at any installation, backtrack if you missed something, and take breaks in a warm café along the way. The trade-off is the cold; two hours outside in December Amsterdam is a real commitment, and some installations are best seen from the water anyway, which a walking route can't fully deliver.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A canal cruise covers the same route sitting down, out of the wind, usually with drinks included in the ticket price. It's the more comfortable option and arguably the more complete one, since several installations are designed to be viewed from the water. The downside is cost and a fixed schedule rather than wandering at will.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>My honest take: if you're only doing this once, the cruise is worth it. If you're staying long enough to do it twice, walk it once and cruise it once — the two views are different enough to justify both.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 id="when-to-go">When to Go</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Go after dark — the installations are essentially invisible in daylight, so timing this for a normal daytime sightseeing slot wastes the whole point. Weeknights tend to be calmer than weekends, and since the festival runs nearly two months, there's no need to force it into your first or last night if a quieter evening fits your schedule better mid-trip.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 id="where-to-stay">Where to Stay</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Basing yourself in or near the historic canal belt or the Jordaan puts you right on or near the Water Colors route, so you can step out for the evening without a long tram ride each way. See my <a href="/where-to-stay-in-amsterdam/">where to stay in Amsterdam guide</a> for a full neighborhood breakdown.</p>
