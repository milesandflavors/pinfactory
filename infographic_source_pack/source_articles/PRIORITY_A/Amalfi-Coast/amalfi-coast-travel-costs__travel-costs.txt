<p>The Amalfi Coast is expensive: genuinely, noticeably, sometimes shockingly expensive. A hotel in Positano in July can run $500-1,500 per night. A seafood dinner with views can cost $100 per person without blinking. A private boat tour can run $400. But the part that keeps this trip from being impossible is simple: many of the experiences that make the Amalfi Coast worth visiting are free. The hiking, the sunsets, the medieval villages, swimming in the clearest water in Italy: all free. This guide breaks down what everything costs so you can spend where it matters and save everywhere else.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="accommodation-costs-on-the-amalfi-coast">Accommodation Costs on the Amalfi Coast</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Accommodation is the single biggest variable in Amalfi Coast travel costs — and where you base yourself makes an enormous difference. The same trip, based in Praiano, Maiori, Minori, or Sorrento instead of Positano, can be much easier to budget without cutting the views, ferries, food, or day trips that actually make the coast worth visiting.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="budget-60-120-night">Budget: $60–120/night</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Budget options on the Amalfi Coast proper are limited — the terrain and land scarcity mean there simply are not many cheap hotels. The most affordable bases are usually Sorrento, Maiori, and Minori, with occasional simple apartments or B&amp;Bs in Amalfi town and Praiano. For Sorrento accommodation at this price point, <a href="https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=Sorrento+Italy" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer">browse options on Booking.com here.</a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="mid-range-150-300-night">Mid-range: $150–300/night</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This is the sweet spot for most travelers — small hotels or apartments with sea views in Amalfi town, Praiano, Maiori, Minori, or Sorrento, and simpler rooms in Positano if you book early. This budget is where you start seeing rooms with a view or terrace without jumping into luxury rates. <!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="is-the-amalfi-coast-worth-the-cost">Is the Amalfi Coast Worth the Cost?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes — but it requires honest expectation management going in. The Amalfi Coast is genuinely expensive, and the most famous parts, especially Positano in July and August, are the most expensive. The coast is also genuinely extraordinary — more beautiful in person than in photographs, which says a lot.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Worth it if:</strong> you're willing to make strategic choices — base yourself in Praiano rather than Positano, visit in shoulder season, use the ferry and bus rather than taxis, and eat one street back from the water. Make those choices and the coast becomes far more manageable.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Less ideal if:</strong> you arrive in peak August expecting it to be affordable without advance planning. The combination of limited accommodation supply and extraordinary demand means last-minute decisions are always the most expensive ones on the Amalfi Coast.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="cost-choices-worth-prioritizing">Cost Choices Worth Prioritizing</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Base in Praiano instead of Positano if the goal is lower accommodation costs. The views are still spectacular, the atmosphere is calmer, and the savings can be significant compared with staying in Positano for the full trip.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Book the boat tour early if it is one of your planned splurges. Smaller-group and better-value options can sell out before peak dates, especially in summer.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Plan at least one meal in Atrani or Minori instead of defaulting to Positano waterfront restaurants. These smaller towns are better places to keep food costs under control.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Bring a small cash buffer, then use bank ATMs in Amalfi town, Positano, or Sorrento where possible. Airport exchange counters are usually the worst place to get euros.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="money-saving-tips-for-the-amalfi-coast">Money Saving Tips for the Amalfi Coast</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="1-stay-in-praiano-instead-of-positano">1. Stay in Praiano instead of Positano</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Praiano offers dramatic sea views and a calmer atmosphere than Positano, often at a noticeably lower accommodation cost. For a week-long trip, that difference can be enough to fund a boat tour, a Capri day, or several good dinners, especially if you book early and keep free cancellation until your plans are stable.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="2-visit-in-shoulder-season">2. Visit in shoulder season</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>May, early June, late September, and October usually offer warmer weather than winter, lighter crowds than peak summer, and better hotel value than July or August. September is the easiest month to love: the sea is still warm from summer, the light is beautiful, and rates often soften after the August peak.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="3-use-the-sita-bus-instead-of-taxis">3. Use the SITA bus instead of taxis</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A bus journey is usually only a few euros, while taxis and private transfers can become expensive quickly on short coast hops. For a week of daily transport, using the bus and ferry for ordinary daytime moves can save a lot compared with defaulting to taxis.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="4-eat-in-atrani-not-positano">4. Eat in Atrani, not Positano</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Atrani's proximity to Amalfi, about 10 minutes on foot, makes it an easy choice for lunch or dinner when Amalfi's most central restaurants feel too tourist-priced. The atmosphere is quieter and more local, and menu prices are often lower than at the most visible waterfront spots.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="5-book-accommodation-early-with-free-cancellation">5. Book accommodation early with free cancellation</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Book early with free cancellation when possible, then check prices again before the cancellation deadline. If rates drop or a better town becomes available, rebook. This is one of the most reliable budgeting strategies for the Amalfi Coast because accommodation is the biggest variable.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="6-take-the-ferry-not-a-private-transfer">6. Take the ferry, not a private transfer</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For arrival day, compare train, ferry, bus, and transfer combinations before paying for a private ride all the way to the coast. A private transfer can be worth it with luggage or late arrivals, but ferry-and-bus or train-and-ferry combinations are usually much cheaper when schedules line up.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="7-do-the-path-of-the-gods">7. Do the Path of the Gods</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Path of the Gods hike costs very little beyond the bus fare and gives you some of the best views on the coast. Do the hike and a boat tour if budget allows — they show you completely different versions of the Amalfi Coast. If you need to choose one free/low-cost highlight, the hike is the obvious pick.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="8-beach-day-at-maiori-or-minori">8. Beach day at Maiori or Minori</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Beach clubs in Positano can be expensive, especially in peak season. Maiori and Minori have wider beaches, more space, and free or lower-cost sections that make more sense for a full beach day. Save the Positano experience for swimming, photos, and atmosphere rather than paying for an entire day on the most famous stretch of sand.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="best-time-to-visit-the-amalfi-coast-for-lower-costs">Best Time to Visit the Amalfi Coast for Lower Costs</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>May–early June:</strong> Warmer weather, a greener coast, and much easier crowds than July or August. Swimming can be possible, though sea temperature depends on the year. This is my first recommendation for a first visit if you want lower stress and better value.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>September–October:</strong> The sea is usually warmer than spring, prices often soften after August, and the light in September is extraordinary. October can be excellent value, but ferry schedules and weather become more variable as the month goes on.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>July–August:</strong> Most expensive and most crowded. If this is your only window, book everything 4–6 months ahead and start every day early to beat the day-tripper crowds.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="practical-money-info">Practical Money Info</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="payment-methods">Payment methods</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Cards are accepted at most restaurants, hotels, and larger shops. Smaller establishments, markets, and some beach clubs prefer cash. Always carry €20–30 in cash for situations where cards aren't accepted.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="at-ms-and-travel-cards">ATMs and travel cards</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>ATMs are available in Amalfi town, Positano, and Sorrento. Avoid airport exchange counters and use bank ATMs in town where possible. For avoiding foreign transaction fees, compare options such as Charles Schwab, <a href="https://wise.com" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer">Wise</a>, Chase Sapphire, and Capital One Venture based on your country and banking setup. For a full breakdown of travel cards and money strategy, our <a href="/best-travel-money-card/">travel money card guide</a> covers this in detail.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="tipping">Tipping</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Tipping is not mandatory in Italy, but rounding up or leaving 5–10% for genuinely good service is appreciated in tourist areas. Some restaurants add a "coperto" or cover charge, which is not the same as a service tip. Check your bill before deciding whether to leave anything extra.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="taxes">Taxes</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Italian VAT (IVA) is included in displayed prices. Many towns charge a "tassa di soggiorno" or tourist tax per person per night, usually paid separately at the property and not always included in the booking headline price. Factor this in when comparing accommodation costs.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="connectivity-costs">Connectivity costs</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Data roaming charges can quietly add to your trip budget. An eSIM can be the easiest option if your home plan is expensive abroad — <a href="https://www.airalo.com/italy-esim" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer">Airalo Italy plans</a> are one option to compare. Set it up before you leave so you are not trying to sort mobile data at the airport.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="travel-insurance">Travel insurance</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Travel insurance cost depends on your country, trip length, medical coverage, and cancellation needs. It is worth comparing carefully for the Amalfi Coast because boat trips, ferry delays, cliff paths, and expensive accommodation can all matter if something changes. <a href="https://www.worldnomads.com/travel-insurance/" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer">World Nomads</a> is one option to compare, but always read the policy wording for your specific trip.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="final-thoughts-amalfi-coast-travel-costs">Final Thoughts: Amalfi Coast Travel Costs</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Amalfi Coast is expensive — but the best experiences here are largely free, and with the right choices the trip is more manageable than its reputation suggests. Stay in Praiano instead of Positano, visit in shoulder season, use the bus and ferry instead of taxis, eat in Atrani instead of on the Positano waterfront. The views, the water, and the hiking are identical regardless of what you're spending.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The one splurge worth protecting in the budget is a boat tour. From the water, the coast reveals itself completely, and that perspective is hard to replace with another activity.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="how-much-does-the-amalfi-coast-cost-per-day">How much does the Amalfi Coast cost per day?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A comfortable mid-range day on the Amalfi Coast (excluding accommodation) costs approximately $135–180 — covering breakfast, transport by ferry and bus, one paid attraction, lunch, and dinner at a good restaurant. Budget travelers can get by on $25–45 per day by focusing on the coast's excellent free experiences and eating at local spots rather than tourist restaurants. Accommodation adds $150–300 per night at mid-range.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="is-the-amalfi-coast-expensive-compared-to-the-rest-of-italy">Is the Amalfi Coast expensive compared to the rest of Italy?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes — it is more expensive than many Italian destinations, particularly for accommodation in Positano, Ravello, and Capri during peak season. Rome, Florence, Naples, and many parts of Puglia usually give you more room choice for the same money. The trade-off is that the scenery, the water, and the hiking are genuinely extraordinary and many of the best moments are free once you are there.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="what-is-the-cheapest-way-to-get-around-the-amalfi-coast">What is the cheapest way to get around the Amalfi Coast?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The SITA bus is usually the cheapest option for moving between towns, though tickets depend on your route and whether you need to change. Ferries cost more but are faster in summer traffic and far more scenic; Travelmar's 2026 fare sheet lists Amalfi–Positano at €10 each way. Avoid taxis for standard town-to-town journeys unless you have luggage, late arrivals, or mobility needs.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="what-is-the-cheapest-town-to-stay-on-the-amalfi-coast">What is the cheapest town to stay on the Amalfi Coast?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Sorrento usually offers the best combination of affordability and connectivity, especially if you want trains, ferries, and more hotel inventory. On the coast itself, Maiori and Minori are usually the best-value bases, followed by Praiano and Amalfi town depending on dates. Positano is usually the most expensive coastal base, while Ravello and Capri skew luxury.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="what-is-the-best-month-to-visit-the-amalfi-coast-on-a-budget">What is the best month to visit the Amalfi Coast on a budget?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>May, early June, late September, and early October usually offer the best balance of weather, lower crowds, and better value than peak summer. September has the advantage of warmer sea temperatures, while May and early June often feel calmer. Avoid July and August if budget is a priority because these are usually the most expensive and crowded months.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
