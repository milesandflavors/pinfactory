<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="before-you-go-car-rental-in-southern-italy">Before You Go — Car Rental in Southern Italy</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A car is non-negotiable for this itinerary. Public transport exists between major cities, but it won't get you to the hidden beaches, the hilltop villages, or the viewpoints that make Southern Italy what it is.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Where to pick up:</strong> Naples International Airport (NAP) is the logical starting point. Pick up your car when you're ready to leave Naples on Day 3, not on arrival — you don't need it in the city, and parking will cost you money and stress.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>What to rent:</strong> Get a small car. Roads in Southern Italy — especially in Puglia towns and on the Amalfi Coast — are narrow in ways that will genuinely concern you if you're driving something large. Manual transmission is standard and significantly cheaper than automatic.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Licence note:</strong> If your driving licence was issued outside the EU/EEA, Italy generally requires an International Driving Permit or an official Italian translation alongside your original licence. Check the <a target="_blank" href="https://www.aci.it/fileadmin/documenti/viaggia_con_noi/pdf/Driving_in_Italy__information_for_visiting_motorists/Car-renting-N.pdf" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer">ACI car-rental guidance</a> before you leave home.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>ZTL zones:</strong> Many Italian town centers are ZTL (limited traffic zones) — driving into them triggers automatic fines that follow you home. Always park outside the historic center and walk in. If you're traveling with a baby or toddler, note that car seats fly free as checked baggage on most airlines — worth bringing your own rather than paying rental rates.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Amalfi Coast road rules:</strong> The SS163 Amalfitana can have seasonal alternate-plate restrictions and local traffic controls in peak periods. For 2026 rules, check the <a target="_blank" href="https://distrettocostadamalfi.it/ordinanza-anas-targhe-alterne-in-costiera-amalfitana-come-funziona/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer">Costa d'Amalfi tourism district traffic guidance</a> before deciding whether to drive between towns or use ferries/buses.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-1-2-naples">Day 1–2: Naples</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-1-morning">Day 1 — Morning</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Start at <strong>Caffè Gambrinus</strong> on Piazza Trieste e Trento — Naples' most historic café, open since 1860. Order a cornetto and a cappuccino and watch the city wake up around you. Naples moves at its own pace and this is the right way to meet it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The historic center of Naples is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most visually intense urban environments in Europe. Walk the full length of <strong>Spaccanapoli</strong> — the long straight street that cuts the city in half. Every block is different. <strong>Naples Cathedral (Duomo)</strong> is worth stepping inside — the Chapel of San Gennaro, with its silver baroque interior, is genuinely extraordinary and free. The <strong>National Archaeological Museum</strong> is one of the finest in the world, housing treasures from Pompeii and Herculaneum.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> Naples rewards wandering more than it rewards planning. Don't rush.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Spaccanapoli-1024x685.jpg" alt="Spaccanapoli street in Naples with laundry hanging between buildings and locals going about their day"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Spaccanapoli — walk the full length and let Naples surprise you.</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-1-afternoon-evening">Day 1 — Afternoon &amp; Evening</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Lunch:</strong> <strong>Sorbillo</strong> on Via dei Tribunali. One of the most famous pizzerias in Naples and the wait is worth it — a Margherita here is as good as pizza gets anywhere in the world.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Afternoon:</strong> Walk to the waterfront at <strong>Lungomare Caracciolo</strong> for views of the Bay of Naples and Mount Vesuvius. <strong>Castel dell'Ovo</strong> — the egg-shaped castle jutting into the sea — is one of the classic waterfront stops, but check current opening status before planning around going inside.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Dinner:</strong> Head to the <strong>Quartieri Spagnoli</strong> — tiny restaurants, loud conversations, and food that has nothing to do with the tourist trail. This is Naples at its most authentic.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Price:</strong> Budget dinner ~$15–20 per person | Mid-range ~$35–50 per person</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Castel-dellOvo-1-1024x683.jpg" alt="Castel dell'Ovo at sunset with the Bay of Naples and Mount Vesuvius visible in the background"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Castel dell'Ovo — check current opening before planning around an inside visit.</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-2-morning-afternoon">Day 2 — Morning &amp; Afternoon</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Spend the second morning underground. <strong>Naples Sotterranea (Naples Underground)</strong> is one of the best experiences in the city — a guided tour through tunnels, cisterns, and wartime shelters beneath the streets. Check current times on the <a target="_blank" href="https://www.napolisotterranea.org/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer">official Napoli Sotterranea route</a>, or use <a target="_blank" href="https://www.viator.com/tours/Naples/Tour-Napoli-Sotterranea-con-guida/d508-88264P1?pid=P00300034&amp;mcid=42383&amp;medium=link&amp;campaign=southern-italy" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Viator for guided Naples Underground options</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The <strong>National Archaeological Museum</strong> in the afternoon — allow 2–3 hours. The collection from Pompeii alone is worth the trip, and seeing it before you visit the ruins in person makes Pompeii dramatically more meaningful. <a target="_blank" href="https://www.viator.com/tours/Naples/Archaeological-Museum-of-Naples-Small-Group-tour-with-an-archaeologist/d508-23258P159?pid=P00300034&amp;mcid=42383&amp;medium=link&amp;campaign=southern-italy" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Book a guided museum tour here.</a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> Leave Naples after lunch on Day 2 — the drive to Sorrento takes about an hour and gives you a relaxed late afternoon arrival.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-3-4-pompeii-sorrento">Day 3–4: Pompeii &amp; Sorrento</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-3-morning-pompeii">Day 3 — Morning: Pompeii</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Pompeii is one of those experiences that exceeds every expectation. Walking through a complete Roman city frozen in 79 AD — the streets, the bakeries, the houses, the graffiti still on the walls — is genuinely overwhelming. The highlights are the Forum, the Villa of the Mysteries, the amphitheater, and the plaster cast figures in the Garden of the Fugitives.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Tickets:</strong> Official Pompeii tickets are sold through the ticket portal linked from <a target="_blank" href="https://pompeiisites.org/en/buy-tickets/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer">Pompeii Sites</a>; guided tours and timed third-party options cost more. <strong>Best time:</strong> First thing at opening — very crowded by 11am | <strong>Time needed:</strong> 3–4 hours</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> Wear comfortable shoes and bring water. The site is large and there is almost no shade. If you want the history to really come alive, going with an archaeologist guide transforms the experience. <a target="_blank" href="https://www.viator.com/tours/Naples/Daily-Pompeii-Group-Tour-with-Archaeological-Guide/d508-18971P2?pid=P00300034&amp;mcid=42383&amp;medium=link&amp;campaign=southern-italy" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">See guided Pompeii tour options here.</a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Mount-Vesuvius-Vesuvio-1-1024x683.jpg" alt="Pompeii ruins with Mount Vesuvius visible in the background on a clear day"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Pompeii with Vesuvius behind — arrive at 9am sharp and you'll have it almost to yourself for the first hour.</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-3-afternoon-sorrento">Day 3 — Afternoon: Sorrento</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After Pompeii, drive to Sorrento — about 30 minutes. Walk through the old center, browse the lemon shops on Corso Italia, and find a terrace with views over the Bay of Naples. Dinner at the waterfront restaurants near Marina Grande — fresh seafood, reasonable prices, and views that justify every dollar.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> Sorrento is the best base for the next day's activities — central, well-connected, and significantly cheaper than the Amalfi Coast towns. <a target="_blank" href="https://expedia.tpm.lv/QJTcy6U9" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Majestic Palace Hotel</a> is a well-reviewed, central option.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-4-day-trip-capri-or-vesuvius">Day 4 — Day Trip: Capri or Vesuvius</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Option A: Capri</strong> — The ferry from Sorrento takes about 25 minutes when conditions and schedules line up. Capri is expensive and crowded in peak summer, but the Blue Grotto, the boat tour around the island, and the views from Monte Solaro are genuinely extraordinary. Go first thing in the morning to beat the day-trippers, check ferry times before locking the day, and compare <a target="_blank" href="https://www.viator.com/tours/Sorrento/Capri-shared-boat-tour-from-Sorrento/d947-152807P5?pid=P00300034&amp;mcid=42383&amp;medium=link&amp;campaign=southern-italy" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Capri boat tour options here</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Option B: Mount Vesuvius</strong> — Drive up toward the crater of the volcano that buried Pompeii. Crater access is ticketed and capacity can change, so check the <a target="_blank" href="https://www.vesuviusnationalpark.it/en/tickets" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer">official Vesuvius National Park ticket page</a> before you go. A guided tour adds significant context to what you are seeing. <a target="_blank" href="https://www.viator.com/tours/Naples/Mount-Vesuvius-hike-and-Pompeii-guided-tour-from-Naples/d508-23258P184?pid=P00300034&amp;mcid=42383&amp;medium=link&amp;campaign=southern-italy" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Book a Vesuvius guided tour here.</a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-5-7-puglia">Day 5–7: Puglia</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-5-polignano-a-mare">Day 5 — Polignano a Mare</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Leave Sorrento after breakfast — the drive to Puglia takes about 3.5 hours. Polignano a Mare is one of those places that looks almost too beautiful to be real — a small town perched on dramatic limestone cliffs above the Adriatic Sea, whitewashed buildings stacked right to the edge, crystal-clear water in coves far below.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Walk through the old town, find the main terrace at <strong>Terrazza Santo Stefano</strong> for the classic view, then work your way down to <strong>Cala Porto (Lama Monachile)</strong> — the small beach tucked into the cliff below. Swimming here with the town rising above you is one of the best moments on the entire itinerary.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> Arrive before 3pm to get a parking spot without stress. For a splurge with a view, <a target="_blank" href="https://expedia.tpm.lv/EakRHbCK" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Grotta Palazzese Beach Hotel</a> is built directly into the cliff, right in town.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Polignano-a-Mare-1024x768.jpg" alt="Polignano a Mare from above showing white buildings on limestone cliffs above brilliant turquoise water"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Polignano a Mare — the view from the cliff terrace is as dramatic as it looks in photos.</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-6-alberobello-ostuni">Day 6 — Alberobello &amp; Ostuni</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Morning: Alberobello</strong> — Drive 40 minutes inland to this UNESCO World Heritage Site famous for its trulli — ancient whitewashed houses with conical stone roofs that look like something from a fairy tale. Nothing else in the world looks like this. Arrive early — tour buses start arriving by 10am. Walk through the <strong>Rione Monti</strong> neighborhood, visit <strong>Trullo Sovrano</strong> (the only two-story trullo), and take your time.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Price:</strong> Free to walk / Trullo Sovrano ~$3 | <strong>Time needed:</strong> 2–3 hours | <strong>Tip:</strong> Staying overnight in Alberobello — some guesthouses in town are converted trulli — is one of the most unique accommodation experiences in Italy and not as expensive as you'd expect. <a target="_blank" href="https://expedia.tpm.lv/iXjJU5Ar" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Hotel Ramapendula</a> is a well-reviewed base right in town.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Afternoon: Cisternino &amp; Ostuni</strong> — Drive 20 minutes to <strong>Cisternino</strong> for lunch. The bombette (stuffed pork rolls cooked on an open grill) from any of the butcher-restaurants on the main street are extraordinary — one of the best lunches on the whole route. From Cisternino, 30 minutes to <strong>Ostuni — the White City</strong>. This hilltop town glowing brilliant white above the olive groves is one of the most beautiful sights in Southern Italy.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For the most memorable base in Puglia, a masseria near Ostuni — a traditional fortified farmhouse, often with a pool and olive groves — is hard to beat. <a target="_blank" href="https://expedia.tpm.lv/x2BIXWKD" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">La Piana degli Ulivi</a>, set in the countryside among the olive trees just outside town, fits that brief well.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Alberobello-731x1024.jpg" alt="Alberobello trulli houses with conical stone roofs catching the morning light"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Alberobello's trulli — arrive early and you'll have the streets almost to yourself.</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-7-beach-day-puglia-coast">Day 7 — Beach Day: Puglia Coast</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Day 7 is a deliberate slow day — beaches, swimming, and doing very little. <strong>Punta Prosciutto</strong> (45 minutes from Ostuni) is one of the best beaches in Italy — long stretches of white sand, shallow clear water, and significantly fewer people than the famous Amalfi beaches.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> Bring your own food and drinks — the beach bars are expensive and the picnic approach is infinitely more enjoyable. This is also a fantastic family beach — the water is very shallow for a long way out, perfect if you're traveling with small children.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Optional addition:</strong> <strong>Lecce</strong> (1 hour from Ostuni) is one of the most beautiful baroque cities in Europe — a compact old town covered in extraordinary stone carvings that earned it the nickname "the Florence of the South." Worth at least 2–3 hours if you have the energy.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-8-matera">Day 8: Matera</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Nothing fully prepares you for your first view of Matera. A city literally carved into the sides of a ravine, with thousands of years of human habitation written into the stone. Matera is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world — the Sassi cave districts were inhabited for around 9,000 years. Walking through them today, with restaurants and boutique hotels carved into the same rock where prehistoric families lived, is one of the most extraordinary experiences in Italy.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Morning:</strong> Drive straight to the <strong>Belvedere di Murgia Timone</strong> viewpoint on the opposite side of the ravine for the first view — the full panorama of the Sassi from across the canyon is breathtaking.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Afternoon:</strong> Cross into the Sassi and wander. The two cave districts — <strong>Sasso Caveoso and Sasso Barisano</strong> — are a maze of staircases, tunnels, cave churches, and extraordinary views. The <strong>Chiesa Rupestre di Santa Maria di Idris</strong>, built directly into the rock, is the highlight.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Price:</strong> Free to walk / Cave church combo ~$5 | <strong>Time needed:</strong> Full day | <strong>Guided tour:</strong> ~$25–35 and genuinely worth it — the history is so dense that context transforms what you're seeing. <a target="_blank" href="https://www.viator.com/tours/Matera/Walking-Tour/d22632-137227P1?pid=P00300034&amp;mcid=42383&amp;medium=link&amp;campaign=southern-italy" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Book a Matera guided tour here.</a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> Stay overnight — Matera at night, with the cave city illuminated across the ravine, is one of the most beautiful sights in Italy. A cave hotel in the Sassi is an experience unlike anything else on this road trip. <a target="_blank" href="https://expedia.tpm.lv/sHJPZlni" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Locanda di San Martino</a>, built into the Sassi with its own Roman-style thermal baths, is exactly that kind of stay.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-9-10-calabria">Day 9–10: Calabria</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-9-tropea">Day 9 — Tropea</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Drive 3 hours from Matera. Tropea is one of the most beautiful coastal towns in Italy and one of the most overlooked — the town sits on a high cliff above a sweep of white sand and extraordinarily clear turquoise water. The <strong>Santuario di Santa Maria dell'Isola</strong>, a church built on a rocky outcrop at the edge of the beach, is the iconic image of the town.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Walk through the old town, work your way down to the beach, and stay for dinner — Tropea is famous for its sweet red onions, which appear in everything and somehow make everything better.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> Stay in the old town if you can — waking up to those cliff views is worth the slightly higher price. <a target="_blank" href="https://expedia.tpm.lv/UWlJJBov" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Townhouse Tropea</a> is a well-reviewed option right in the historic center. To see the coastline from the water, a boat trip and snorkeling tour from Tropea is excellent value. <a target="_blank" href="https://www.viator.com/tours/Tropea/Boat-Tour-along-the-Coast-of-the-Gods-with-Snorkeling/d33165-410852P1?pid=P00300034&amp;mcid=42383&amp;medium=link&amp;campaign=southern-italy" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">See Tropea boat tour options here.</a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-10-scilla">Day 10 — Scilla</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Drive 1.5 hours south to Scilla — less famous than Tropea and better for it. Explore <strong>Chianalea</strong> — the lower village where the houses are built so close to the sea that fishing boats moor directly outside front doors. Walk up to <strong>Castello Ruffo</strong> for views over the Strait of Messina — on a clear day you can see Sicily, just three kilometers away.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> Try the swordfish — Scilla is famous for it. Staying right on the waterfront in Chianalea is one of the most atmospheric nights on the entire route. <a target="_blank" href="https://expedia.tpm.lv/rsbNjASA" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Signora Tita B&B</a> puts you right in Chianalea, steps from the water.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Scilla-1-1024x683.jpg" alt="Chianalea fishing village in Scilla with colorful houses built right on the water's edge"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Chianalea in Scilla — one of the most atmospheric places on the entire route, and almost nobody comes here.</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-11-maratea">Day 11: Maratea</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Drive 2.5 hours north to Maratea — the place on this itinerary that almost nobody knows about, and the one that almost everyone ends up calling their favorite. Often called "the Pearl of the Tyrrhenian Sea," Maratea sits on the Tyrrhenian coast in a landscape of dramatic cliffs and deep blue water that earned it the name.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Morning:</strong> Drive up to the <strong>Cristo Redentore statue</strong> — a 21-meter white marble figure on the peak above the town, with panoramic views of the entire coastline that are among the most extraordinary on this route.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Afternoon:</strong> Find a beach. <strong>Spiaggia Marina di Castrocucco</strong> is one of the most beautiful — a small rocky cove with remarkably clear water.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> Maratea is the perfect place to slow down before the final push to the Amalfi Coast. Don't rush through it — it's the kind of place you'll wish you'd given an extra day. <a target="_blank" href="https://expedia.tpm.lv/ywECPZ0I" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Hotel Murmann</a> is a good-value, well-reviewed base here.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-12-14-amalfi-coast">Day 12–14: Amalfi Coast</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-12-arrival-positano">Day 12 — Arrival &amp; Positano</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The drive from Maratea takes about 2.5 hours. By the time you arrive, you've seen enough of Southern Italy to understand why the Amalfi Coast is famous — and to appreciate it properly rather than simply photographing it. That's the point of saving it for last.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Afternoon:</strong> Walk down through Positano's colorful streets to the <strong>Spiaggia Grande</strong> beach. Find a spot for an aperitivo as the sun gets lower over the sea.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Driving tip:</strong> On the Amalfi Coast road, go early or go by ferry. The road is narrow, the buses are large, summer traffic can mean hours of waiting, and seasonal SS163 restrictions may affect rental cars. The ferry between towns is often faster and gives you the best views of the coastline.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Positano-1-683x1024.jpg" alt="Positano from above showing colorful buildings cascading down the cliffside to the beach and blue sea"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Positano — everything you've seen in photos, and better in person. Save it for Day 12 and you'll appreciate it the way it deserves.</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-13-ravello-amalfi-the-path-of-the-gods">Day 13 — Ravello, Amalfi &amp; The Path of the Gods</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Morning: Ravello</strong> — Drive up to Ravello — 10 minutes above Amalfi town, sitting at 350 meters above the sea. <strong>Villa Cimbrone's Terrace of Infinity</strong> is one of the most celebrated viewpoints in Italy — a row of marble busts at the edge of a cliff with the sea stretching to the horizon far below. Come in the morning before the tour groups arrive.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Price:</strong> Villa Cimbrone ~$8 / Villa Rufolo ~$8 | <strong>Time needed:</strong> 2 hours</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Afternoon: Path of the Gods</strong> — If you only choose one active experience on the Amalfi Coast, make it this. The hiking trail runs along the clifftops above Positano with views of the entire coast that no photograph does justice to. The classic route runs from Agerola to Nocelle and takes 3–4 hours one way.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Best time:</strong> Early morning in summer | <strong>Difficulty:</strong> Moderate | <strong>Tip:</strong> Take the bus from Amalfi to Agerola to start, walk down to Positano, take the ferry back.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A guided hike adds local stories and context that transform the experience. <a target="_blank" href="https://www.viator.com/Naples-attractions/Path-of-the-Gods-Sentiero-Degli-Dei/d508-a25933?pid=P00300034&amp;mcid=42383&amp;medium=link&amp;campaign=southern-italy" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Book a guided Path of the Gods hike here.</a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-14-boat-tour-farewell">Day 14 — Boat Tour &amp; Farewell</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The last day of this Southern Italy road trip should be slow. You've earned it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A boat tour along the Amalfi Coast is the best way to end the trip — from the water, you see the entire coastline you've been exploring from a completely new angle, including grottos, hidden beaches, and sea caves that can't be reached any other way. A private boat gives full flexibility and is worth splitting costs for a small group; a shared tour is excellent value if you're happy to go with others.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://www.viator.com/tours/Sorrento/Capri-Blue-Grotto-Small-Group-Boat-Day-Tour-From-Sorrento/d947-42612P9?pid=P00300034&amp;mcid=42383&amp;medium=link&amp;campaign=southern-italy" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Book a private Amalfi Coast boat tour here.</a> | <a target="_blank" href="https://www.viator.com/tours/Salerno/From-Salerno-Small-Group-Amalfi-Coast-boat-tour-with-stops-in-Positano-and-Amalfi/d4736-51921P55?pid=P00300034&amp;mcid=42383&amp;medium=link&amp;campaign=southern-italy" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">See shared boat tour options here.</a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Afternoon:</strong> Return to Amalfi town for a final long lunch by the harbor. Order the spaghetti alle vongole, drink the local white wine, take your time. The drive back to Naples airport takes about 1.5 hours — allow 2 with traffic, more in summer.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> If your flight is early the next morning, stay overnight in Naples rather than driving the Amalfi Coast road in the dark.</p>

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="is-southern-italy-worth-it">Is Southern Italy Worth It?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes — if you like road trips with real contrast. Southern Italy is one of the most underrated travel regions in Europe because it does not rely on one famous coastline. The Amalfi Coast may be the headline, but Matera, Tropea, Maratea, Naples, and Puglia give the route its depth.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>It's worth it if</strong> you love food, history, dramatic coastlines, and destinations that feel genuinely different from each other. Every stop on this route has a completely distinct character — from Naples' chaotic energy to Matera's eerie timelessness to the laid-back pace of Calabria.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>It's less ideal if</strong> you need everything to be easy and polished. Southern Italy rewards patience and flexibility — roads can be narrow, signs confusing, and plans sometimes need adjusting. That's part of what makes it extraordinary.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="smart-route-adjustments">Smart Route Adjustments</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Book Amalfi Coast accommodation earlier than feels necessary — for June to September, 4–6 months ahead is safer than 3 months if you want Praiano or Positano.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Add an extra night in Matera. One night is enough to see it, but two nights lets you experience it properly — including the illuminated cave city at midnight when all the day visitors are gone.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Do not skip Maratea if the route allows it. It breaks up the long Calabria-to-Amalfi drive and adds a quieter coastal stop before the busiest part of the trip.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Take the ferry between Amalfi Coast towns instead of driving wherever possible — it's faster, more relaxing, and the views from the water are extraordinary.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="best-time-for-a-southern-italy-road-trip">Best Time for a Southern Italy Road Trip</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Spring (May–early June)</strong> is the ideal time — warm weather (70–80°F), clear seas, far fewer crowds than summer, and lower prices on accommodation. The landscape is green, the wildflowers are out, and every outdoor experience is better without summer heat and crowds.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Fall (September–October)</strong> is equally excellent — the sea is still warm from summer, prices drop significantly after August, and the September light is extraordinary for photography. For a first Southern Italy road trip, September is the strongest overall balance of weather, prices, and crowd levels.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Summer (July–August)</strong> is peak season — hot (85–100°F in Calabria), crowded, and significantly more expensive. The Amalfi Coast in particular becomes extremely busy. If summer is your only option, book everything months in advance.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Shoulder season tip:</strong> September in Southern Italy is the sweet spot - hotel rates are often less punishing than August, the sea is usually still warm, and the most popular stops feel calmer. The difference in price and experience between August and September can be dramatic on this route.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="practical-info">Practical Info</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="getting-to-naples">Getting to Naples</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Naples International Airport (NAP) has seasonal direct transatlantic flights, including New York-area routes in current schedules, but routes and dates change by year. From other US cities, connect through Rome (FCO) or a European hub. <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Is 14 days enough for Southern Italy?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes — 14 days is the ideal length for this route. It covers Naples, Puglia, Matera, Calabria, and the Amalfi Coast without feeling rushed, and includes a deliberate slow day in Puglia for beach time. You could do it in 10 days by cutting Calabria, but you'd miss Tropea and Scilla — which would be a genuine shame.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Do I need to speak Italian for this road trip?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>No — English is widely spoken in tourist areas, hotels, and restaurants. In smaller towns and villages like Maratea or Scilla you may encounter less English, but Italians are generally warm and patient with visitors. A few basic phrases (thank you, please, the bill) go a long way.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Is Southern Italy safe for tourists?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For most visitors, the main tourist route through Naples, Puglia, Matera, Calabria, and the Amalfi Coast feels manageable with normal big-city awareness. Naples is more intense than the coastal towns, so keep an eye on your belongings, do not leave valuables in your car, and use the same common sense you would in any busy European city.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>What is the best base on the Amalfi Coast?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Praiano is the strongest value recommendation for this route — it is close to Positano by road or ferry, significantly quieter, and has some of the best views on the coast. Positano is worth one night if your budget allows, but as a base for 3 days it is expensive and the endless stairs can be tiring. Amalfi town is the most practical if you want central access to everything without the premium pricing.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>What is the best month for a Southern Italy road trip?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>September is often the best month - the sea is usually still warm, the coast feels calmer after August, and accommodation can be better value than peak summer. May and early June are close behind. Avoid July and August if you can - the heat in Calabria is intense and the Amalfi Coast becomes genuinely overcrowded.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->
