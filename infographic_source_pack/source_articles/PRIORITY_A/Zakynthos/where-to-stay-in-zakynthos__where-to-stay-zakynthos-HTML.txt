<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="quick-answer-where-to-stay-in-zakynthos">Quick Answer: Where Should You Stay in Zakynthos?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For most first-time visitors, the best place to stay in Zakynthos is Zakynthos Town if you rent a car, Tsilivi if you want an easy family-friendly beach base, and Kalamaki if you want a calmer resort area without being isolated. I would only choose Laganas if nightlife is a priority. For the quietest beaches and nature, look at Vassilikos or Gerakas, but only if you are comfortable renting a car.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="zakynthos-town-our-pick-and-why">Zakynthos Town — Our Pick, and Why</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We stayed in Zakynthos Town and we'd do it again without hesitation. It's the most interesting base on the island — a proper Greek town with a real waterfront, good local restaurants, and a pace that has nothing to do with the tourist strip. If you want to feel like you're actually somewhere rather than inside a resort bubble, this is it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The town itself is worth exploring: the Venetian castle above the old quarter, the main square at Agios Markos, and the harbor lined with boats rather than beach bars. It has character. The rest of the island doesn't always.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Where we stayed: Palatino Hotel.</strong> Breakfast was included and genuinely good — not the standard buffet you'd skip given the option, but something we actually looked forward to. We also had lunch there on a couple of days, and the food held up. The room was spacious with a mountain view. It's quiet, well-run, and the kind of place where nothing feels like a problem. We'd both book it again.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="laganas-the-party-strip-plain-and-simple">Laganas — The Party Strip, Plain and Simple</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Laganas is exactly what it sounds like. In peak season it's one of the loudest resort strips in the Greek islands — beach bars running all day, clubs going until early morning, the whole infrastructure built around a crowd that isn't there to sleep. That's not a criticism. It's a description. If that's the trip you want, Laganas is the right place to have it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The beach itself is genuinely long and sandy — one of the longer stretches on the island, and wide enough that it doesn't feel as chaotic as the streets behind it. The water is clean. The infrastructure is thorough: sun loungers, water sports, everything you'd expect.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>What Laganas is not: a quiet base, a place to escape the crowds, or somewhere that will give you an authentic sense of Greece. It's a resort town that happens to be on a Greek island. Know that going in and it delivers exactly what it promises.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="tsilivi-the-family-friendly-default">Tsilivi — The Family-Friendly Default</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Tsilivi sits north of Zakynthos Town and is the island's main family resort area. It's significantly calmer than Laganas — the nightlife exists but it's low-key, and the crowd skews toward families and couples rather than party groups. The beach is sandy, well-organized, and good for children. Water sports are available, there's a decent range of restaurants, and the whole area is built for comfortable, uncomplicated beach holidays.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It doesn't have the charm of Zakynthos Town or the dramatic natural beauty of the south, but it's reliable. Everything works, it's walkable, and it gives you a decent base even without a car — though you'll still need transport to reach the island's best beaches.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Choose Tsilivi if:</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>You're traveling with young children and want a calm, walkable resort area</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>You want a sandy beach within easy reach of your hotel</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>You want some restaurants and amenities but not a party atmosphere</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="vassilikos-peninsula-and-gerakas-the-quiet-natural-choice">Vassilikos Peninsula and Gerakas — The Quiet, Natural Choice</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Vassilikos peninsula is the quietest and most naturally beautiful part of the island. It stretches south from Zakynthos Town into a series of small beaches, olive groves, and quiet villages. There's almost no nightlife, limited tourist infrastructure, and that's entirely the point. If you want to disconnect, this is where you go.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Gerakas, at the tip of the peninsula, is one of the most important sea turtle nesting beaches in Europe. The beach is genuinely beautiful — broad, pale sand, clear water, backed by low cliffs. Part of the beach is off-limits to protect the nesting areas, but the accessible section is large enough and far less crowded than anything near Laganas or Tsilivi.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Local tip:</strong> The nesting area is fenced off — don't go behind the barriers. But if you visit in late summer during hatching season, you may spot baby turtles making their way to the sea. It's one of those things that stays with you. Worth the drive down.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<li>You want walkable restaurants and evening entertainment</li>

It's calm, the beach is long and uncrowded, and the atmosphere is completely different from the busy resort south. The village is small, quiet, and has a local feel that's hard to find in the more tourist-heavy parts of Zakynthos.</p>

<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Kalamaki sits between Laganas and the quieter southern parts of the island. It's calmer than Laganas but has more going on than Vassilikos — a handful of good restaurants, a sheltered beach, and a pace that suits couples and families who want some amenities without the resort circus.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The beach at Kalamaki is also a turtle nesting site, though less well-known than Gerakas. Swimming is restricted in parts at night during nesting season, but during the day it's a good, uncrowded beach. If you want proximity to Laganas for a night out without actually staying in it, Kalamaki is close enough to manage that without the noise.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Choose Kalamaki if:</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>You want calm evenings but a proper beach within walking distance</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>You're somewhere between wanting a resort and wanting to avoid one</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>You want easy access to Laganas without sleeping inside it</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Avoid Kalamaki if:</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>You want a wide choice of restaurants and entertainment — the village is small</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>You want the most beautiful or dramatic beaches — those are elsewhere</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If you want a quieter resort compromise, <a href="https://www.booking.com" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer">compare Kalamaki hotels on Booking.com</a> and look for properties close to the beach.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="what-wed-do-differently">What We'd Do Differently</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Nothing, in terms of where we stayed. Zakynthos Town and the Palatino Hotel was the right call — we'd book it again without any hesitation. The breakfast alone would be reason enough.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The one thing we'd add: if you're staying in town, book the car at the same time as the hotel. The two decisions are connected — Zakynthos Town works best as a base precisely because a car makes the whole island accessible. Lock in the car early; automatics go fast in peak season.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="the-bottom-line-on-where-to-stay-in-zakynthos">The Bottom Line on Where to Stay in Zakynthos</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Where to stay in Zakynthos comes down to one honest question: are you renting a car? If yes, Zakynthos Town is the best base on the island — genuine character, good food, quiet evenings, and every beach within reach. If no, you need to be near a beach, and the right choice depends on what kind of trip you want: Tsilivi for families, Kalamaki for something calmer, Laganas if nightlife is the point.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Vassilikos and Alikes are worth considering if you specifically want to escape the resort infrastructure. Both reward the effort of getting there.</p>

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="faq-where-to-stay-in-zakynthos">FAQ: Where to Stay in Zakynthos</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="best-area-to-stay-in-zakynthos">What is the best area to stay in Zakynthos?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The best area to stay in Zakynthos depends on whether you rent a car. With a car, Zakynthos Town is the best base for exploring the island. Without a car, Tsilivi or Kalamaki are easier because they have beaches, restaurants, and hotels within walking distance.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="should-you-stay-in-zakynthos-town">Is Zakynthos Town a good place to stay?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes, Zakynthos Town is a great place to stay if you want restaurants, a real local feel, and easy access to the whole island by car. It is not the best choice if you want to walk straight from your hotel to the island's prettiest beach.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="is-laganas-good-for-families">Is Laganas good for families?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Laganas can work for families outside peak party season, but I would not make it my first choice in July or August. Tsilivi, Kalamaki, and Vassilikos are usually better family bases because they are calmer.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="where-to-stay-in-zakynthos-without-car">Where should you stay in Zakynthos without a car?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Without a car, stay somewhere walkable to a beach and restaurants. Tsilivi is the easiest all-round choice, Kalamaki is calmer, and Laganas is best only if nightlife is part of the plan.</p>
