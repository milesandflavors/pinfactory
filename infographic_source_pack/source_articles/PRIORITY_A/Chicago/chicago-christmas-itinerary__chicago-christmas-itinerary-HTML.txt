Christkindlmarket, Millennium Park's skating rink, the Magnificent Mile lights, and ZooLights at Lincoln Park Zoo all sit in different pockets of the city, and trying to hit them in the wrong order means backtracking across the Loop in the cold more than once.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This itinerary sequences two full days around the Loop's free highlights, then adds a third for Lincoln Park and ZooLights if you have the time. For the full decision-support version of this guide — what's free, what's paid, and whether Chicago at Christmas is worth it at all — see <a href="/chicago-at-christmas/">Chicago at Christmas: What Is Actually Worth It</a>.</p>

<h2 id="day-1">Day 1: The Loop, Christkindlmarket, and Millennium Park</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Start in the Loop, where the Christmas core is compact enough to cover on foot. Begin at <strong>Millennium Park</strong> for Cloud Gate and the McCormick Tribune Ice Rink — admission has traditionally been free with an online reservation, while skate rental costs extra, so check the current season's reservation system before you go. Cloud Gate itself is worth lingering at in winter; the reflective surface picks up the skyline and the season's lights differently than any other time of year.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>From Millennium Park, it's a short walk to <strong>Daley Plaza for Christkindlmarket</strong>, the anchor of Chicago's holiday season. Admission is traditionally free; your spending decision is food, drinks, and shopping. Pick one savory item, one sweet item, and a hot drink before deciding whether anything else is worth the line. Weekday afternoons give you more room to browse than weekend evenings, especially close to Christmas.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:image {"id":2694,"sizeSlug":"large","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://milesandflavors.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/chicago-millennium-park-ice-skating-christmas.jpg" alt="Crowded outdoor ice rink at Millennium Park in Chicago with skaters and holiday banners, downtown skyline in the background" class="wp-image-2694"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">The McCormick Tribune Ice Rink at Millennium Park, a natural Day 1 stop before Christkindlmarket.</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<p>Round out the day walking State Street, then warm up over dinner in the Loop before heading back to your hotel. Confirm the current-year Christkindlmarket and Millennium Park skating schedules before you lock in your dates — both were unpublished as of this guide's last check, and Chicago's holiday calendars typically post closer to the season.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 id="day-2">Day 2: The Magnificent Mile and the River</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Day 2 moves north to <strong>the Magnificent Mile</strong>. If your trip lands on or after November 21, 2026, that's the date of the <a href="https://www.themagnificentmile.com/lights-festival" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Magnificent Mile Lights Festival</a> — Lights Festival Lane runs 11am to 4pm, followed by a parade at 5:30pm and a fireworks finale, lighting more than a million lights on 200-plus trees along North Michigan Avenue. If your trip falls after the festival itself, the lights and decorated storefronts are still worth the walk any evening.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Start the walk near the Chicago River and head north toward the historic Water Tower, pacing the distance to the weather. Break for lunch or a warm-up stop partway through — this is a long walk in December, and alternating outdoor blocks with indoor stops keeps the day comfortable rather than a race against the cold.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If your dates allow, an evening beside the Chicago River rounds out Day 2 well — the bridges and skyline lights reflect off the water in a way that's worth the extra half hour outside, even in the cold.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 id="day-3">Day 3 (Optional): Lincoln Park and ZooLights</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Lincoln Park Zoo's <strong>ZooLights</strong> is the reason to add a third day. For 2026, it runs <a href="https://www.lpzoo.org/event/zoolights/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">November 20 through January 3, 2027</a>, from 4:30 to 9pm with last entry at 8pm. Advance fixed-date tickets run $7 to $12, same-day and flexible tickets are $20, and select Mondays are free with a required reservation released a week ahead. Fixed-date tickets are nonrefundable and some dates close for private events, so check the official calendar before paying.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Because the zoo sits north of downtown, pair ZooLights with dinner in Lincoln Park rather than shuttling back to the Loop between plans. Public transit or rideshare tends to be more practical than driving — the zoo's own paid lot is limited and can fill on busy nights.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Think of ZooLights as a light-and-atmosphere event rather than a normal zoo visit; animal viewing isn't the draw after dark. If you're deciding whether it's worth the extra day at all, my <a href="/lincoln-park-zoo-chicago-worth-it/">Lincoln Park Zoo: Is It Worth Your Time?</a> guide covers the daytime version of that same decision.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 id="what-to-pack">What to Pack</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Chicago's December climate normals sit around 36.6°F for the average high and 24.4°F for the average low (2.6°C and -4.2°C), with colder spells possible and wind off Lake Michigan a real factor. Pack an insulated coat, thermal base layers, waterproof footwear with good grip, a hat that covers your ears, a scarf, and touchscreen gloves. For skating, bring your own gloves and comfortable (not overly thick) socks so rented skates still fit well.</p>
