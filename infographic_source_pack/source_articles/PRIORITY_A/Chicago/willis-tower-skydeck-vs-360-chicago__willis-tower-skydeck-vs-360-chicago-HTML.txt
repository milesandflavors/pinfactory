Willis Tower Skydeck sits in the Loop on the 103rd floor, once the tallest building in the world and still the most dramatic way to see Chicago from altitude. 360 Chicago occupies the 94th floor of the John Hancock Center on the Magnificent Mile, pointing you directly toward Lake Michigan from the north end of downtown. Both are worth doing. They're genuinely different experiences — and the right choice depends on what you're after.</p>

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="willis-tower-skydeck">Willis Tower Skydeck: What to Expect</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Willis Tower — most Chicagoans still call it the Sears Tower — was the tallest building in the world from 1973 to 1998. It's no longer the global record holder, but it is still Chicago's tallest building, and the Skydeck on the 103rd floor delivers one of the most complete urban views in the city.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Getting there is part of the experience. You pass through ticketing and a brief security check, then move through exhibit rooms on Chicago's architectural history before a high-speed elevator takes you 103 floors in about 60 seconds. When the doors open, the view is immediate and total. The full Chicago grid stretches west and south, the lakefront runs along the east, and the dense Loop towers rise below you in every direction.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Skydeck floor is spacious, with floor-to-ceiling windows on all four sides and the four Ledge glass boxes set into the south and west walls. It gets busy — weekend afternoons in summer will have you waiting for your Ledge moment. Weekday mornings before 11:00 AM are meaningfully calmer, and the light is better for photos.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="the-ledge">The Ledge: Is It Worth It?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Ledge is what most people come for, and it earns the hype. Four glass boxes extend four feet beyond the building at 1,353 feet above street level. The walls are glass. The floor is glass. You step in, and the Chicago street grid is directly below you — no building, no ledge, no steel, just 103 floors of nothing between you and the Loop.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>What you actually feel is a specific combination of awe and nervous laughter. The glass is engineered to hold weight far beyond anything you'll put on it, but your body doesn't entirely believe that for the first fifteen seconds. Then it does, and you just stand there looking straight down at the city.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Ledge experience is brief — five to ten minutes is usually enough, especially when others are waiting. But it's one of those travel moments you still think about six months later. If you're going to Willis Tower, step into the glass box.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="360-chicago">360 Chicago: What to Expect</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>360 Chicago is on the 94th floor of the John Hancock Center at 875 North Michigan Avenue — right in the middle of the Magnificent Mile. That location is its clearest advantage over Willis Tower. You walk out of the building and you're already on one of Chicago's best shopping and dining streets, and the building is significantly easier to reach on foot from North Side and River North hotels than Willis Tower is from the same area.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The observation deck is smaller and more intimate than Willis Tower's Skydeck, which often means shorter wait times. But the real differentiator is the view east. Lake Michigan stretches to the horizon from up here — genuinely breathtaking and not replicable from Willis Tower. From the Loop, you're looking at the city. From 360 Chicago, you're looking at the city and the lake together, and on a clear day the water goes as far as you can see.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>There's also a bar on the observation floor. The Sip &amp; View ticket starts around $44 online and includes admission plus one item from the Sip &amp; View menu. For anyone who wants to linger rather than rush through and check a box, that's genuinely good value.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="tilt">The TILT Experience</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>TILT is 360 Chicago's signature moment — windows on the east side of the observation deck tilt outward at 30 degrees, so you end up leaning over North Michigan Avenue at roughly 1,000 feet with nothing in front of you but glass and a long way down to the Mag Mile. It is sold either as part of a bundled TILT ticket or as an add-on when available, so check the current ticket options before you decide.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Honest take: TILT is fun and the view straight down at Michigan Avenue is striking, especially if you're standing over the street at an angle for the first time. But the experience is shorter than the Ledge at Willis Tower — about 90 seconds of tilting — and the novelty fades faster. The regular views from 360 Chicago are strong enough on their own that TILT feels like a good bonus rather than a reason to choose one deck over the other.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="which-to-choose">Willis Tower vs 360 Chicago: Which to Choose</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Choose <strong>Willis Tower Skydeck</strong> if:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list {"className":"wp-block-list"} -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>You want the highest observation deck in Chicago</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>The Ledge glass-box experience is on your list</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>You want 360-degree views including west and south over the full Chicago grid</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>You're spending a day in the Loop and want to combine it with the architecture river cruise</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Choose <strong>360 Chicago</strong> if:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list {"className":"wp-block-list"} -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Lake Michigan views are important to you</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>You want a cocktail with your skyline (Sip &amp; View is excellent value)</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>You're staying on the Magnificent Mile and don't want to travel to the Loop</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>You prefer a smaller, less crowded space</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>You want the lower starting online price on many dates — 360 Chicago general admission currently starts from $29 for adults online</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If you can only choose one: <strong>Willis Tower</strong> for most first-time Chicago visitors. The height difference is real, the Ledge is unforgettable, and the full 360-degree view over the Loop is the more complete Chicago-from-above experience. That said, 360 Chicago has a genuine case of its own — the Lake Michigan view from the John Hancock tower is something Willis Tower can't replicate, and the Sip &amp; View experience turns it into something to linger over rather than rush through.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Both in one day is entirely doable if Chicago views are a priority. They're about two miles apart, the experiences are different enough that there's no real overlap, and together they give you Chicago from both ends of downtown.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="practical-tips">Practical Tips: Tickets, Timing, Crowds</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list {"className":"wp-block-list"} -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Reserve ahead if your schedule matters.</strong> Timed-entry windows and ticket tiers vary by date, and busy weekends can add friction. For Willis Tower especially, having the ticket sorted makes the Loop day easier to plan.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Best time for Willis Tower:</strong> weekday morning before 11:00 AM. The Ledge queue is shortest and the light from the east is good for photos.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Best time for 360 Chicago:</strong> late afternoon into evening. The Lake Michigan sunset from the east-facing deck is spectacular, and the Sip &amp; View option fits naturally into an evening on the Magnificent Mile.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Combined visit logistics:</strong> Willis Tower is at 233 S Wacker Dr in the Loop. 360 Chicago is at 875 N Michigan Ave on the Magnificent Mile. About 2 miles apart — easiest by rideshare. Plan 90 minutes per deck including exhibits and wait time.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Photography:</strong> Keep your setup simple and check the current tripod, selfie-stick and bag rules before you go. The Ledge reflection is a classic shot — crouch slightly and shoot through the floor glass at the street below.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Accessibility:</strong> Both decks are fully accessible. Willis Tower has an elevator directly to the 103rd floor. The Ledge has a small raised threshold to step into the glass boxes.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="faq">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Is Willis Tower Skydeck worth it in Chicago?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes — for most first-time Chicago visitors, Willis Tower Skydeck is worth it. The 103rd-floor view gives you the full Chicago grid in every direction, and the Ledge glass-box experience is one of those travel moments that genuinely stays with you. Choose a weekday morning slot if you want a calmer Ledge queue and better light.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>What is the Ledge at Willis Tower Skydeck?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Ledge is four glass boxes that extend four feet beyond the 103rd floor of Willis Tower, 1,353 feet above street level. The walls and floor are transparent, so you're standing on glass with nothing below you but the Chicago street grid. The Ledge is included in the standard Skydeck admission — there's no separate ticket. Expect a short queue on busy days.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>How long should I spend at Willis Tower Skydeck?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Allow 60–90 minutes for a complete Willis Tower Skydeck visit — about 20 minutes for the exhibits on the way up, 30–40 minutes on the observation deck and Ledge, and time for the elevator queue and descent. On busy summer weekends, add another 20–30 minutes for the Ledge specifically.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Is 360 Chicago worth visiting?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes, especially if Lake Michigan views matter to you or you want a more relaxed observation deck experience. 360 Chicago sits on the 94th floor of the John Hancock Center on the Magnificent Mile — the Lake Michigan view from up here is genuinely different from anything Willis Tower offers. The Sip &amp; View ticket (admission plus a cocktail) makes it a good choice for couples or anyone who wants to linger rather than rush.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Can I do both Willis Tower and 360 Chicago in one day?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes — both observation decks in one Chicago day is very doable. They're about two miles apart and best connected by rideshare. Allow 90 minutes per deck. A good sequence: Willis Tower in the morning for the best Ledge light and shorter queues, then 360 Chicago in the late afternoon for the Lake Michigan sunset and the Sip &amp; View experience. Both together gives you the full Chicago-from-above picture.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
