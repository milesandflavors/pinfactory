<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="is-it-worth-it">Is Chicago Worth It for Thanksgiving?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes, for a specific kind of traveler. Chicago's version of Thanksgiving isn't a scaled-down New York — it's its own thing. The city runs its own Thanksgiving Day parade down State Street, the Magnificent Mile Lights Festival flips the switch on the holiday season just days before, and the whole downtown core has a cold, quiet, string-lights atmosphere that's genuinely different from how the city feels in October or even a normal December weekend.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>What it isn't is a normal sightseeing Thursday. Several major museums close, restaurant hours move to holiday schedules, and some seasonal events pause for the evening. Build Thanksgiving Day around the parade, the Skydeck, an outdoor walk if the weather cooperates, and a confirmed dinner reservation.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Save the museum-heavy part of the trip for Friday or the weekend. Lincoln Park Zoo is an exception to the shutdown pattern: the zoo itself is open 365 days a year. ZooLights is a separate ticketed evening event, so verify its 2026 date-by-date calendar before assuming it runs on Thanksgiving night.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="weather-thanksgiving-week">Weather in Chicago Thanksgiving Week</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Late November in Chicago is cold in the way that changes how you plan a day, not just what coat you wear. <a href="https://www.weather.gov/lot/November_Normals_Chicago" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer">National Weather Service climate normals</a> for November 26 are about <strong>43°F for the high and 30°F for the low</strong>. Wind can make exposed stretches along the lake and downtown streets feel colder than the number on your phone suggests.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Snow is possible but not something to count on for the holiday itself; it's more of a broader late-fall-into-winter possibility than a guaranteed Thanksgiving-week backdrop. Pack for genuine cold and wind rather than a specific weather scene, and plan on real indoor stretches during the day — this isn't a week to build an all-outdoor itinerary around.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="thanksgiving-parade">The Chicago Thanksgiving Parade</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Chicago has run its own Thanksgiving Day parade for close to a century, historically known for years as the McDonald's Thanksgiving Parade before shifting to the Chicago Thanksgiving Parade branding it uses today. The 2026 edition is the 92nd annual parade, taking place on <strong>Thursday, November 26, 2026</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The route runs north along State Street, from Ida B. Wells Drive to Randolph Street, with the parade scheduled from <strong>8 a.m. to 11 a.m. Central Time</strong>. Expect marching bands, floats, and balloons in the classic Thanksgiving-parade format. It is smaller than Macy's in New York, but it is a real, long-running local tradition and the clearest reason to be downtown on Thanksgiving morning. For current logistics and any last-minute changes, check the <a href="https://www.choosechicago.com/blog/special-events/chicago-thanksgiving-day-parade/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Choose Chicago parade guide</a> closer to your dates.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="lights-festival-christkindlmarket">The Lights Festival and Christkindlmarket Timing</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The other piece that makes Thanksgiving week feel bigger than the holiday itself is timing: the <a href="https://www.themagnificentmile.com/lights-festival" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer"><strong>Magnificent Mile Lights Festival</strong></a> is scheduled for <strong>Saturday, November 21, 2026</strong>, the Saturday before Thanksgiving. The event brings tree lighting and an evening parade to North Michigan Avenue. If your trip starts a few days before Thanksgiving, you can catch both major events on the same visit.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Christkindlmarket, the German-style holiday market that usually sets up at Daley Plaza, has opened around this part of the calendar in previous years. However, its exact 2026 schedule was not published at the time of this update. Check the <a href="https://www.christkindlmarket.com/special-events/" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer">official Christkindlmarket calendar</a> before treating it as a fixed part of your trip.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="where-to-eat">Where to Eat Thanksgiving Dinner in Chicago</h2>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Treat Thanksgiving dinner like a holiday reservation, not a normal night out. Chicago hotels and independent restaurants often publish dedicated Thanksgiving menus, but the participating venues, prices, and reservation terms change each year.</p>

<p>I would compare the current Thanksgiving listings on a reservation platform, then verify the menu on the restaurant's own website before booking. Reserve earlier than you would for a normal Thursday, especially if you need a specific time, a children's menu, or dietary accommodations.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="hotel-rates">Hotel Rates During Thanksgiving Week</h2>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Compare the exact same dates across the Loop, River North, and nearby neighborhoods. Look at the final total after taxes and destination fees, not just the first nightly rate, and favor flexible terms if your holiday plans are still moving.</p>

Museums generally return to their published schedules, while Lincoln Park Zoo has remained open throughout. Recheck each attraction's calendar because holiday-week hours can still differ from an ordinary weekend.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="who-should-come">Who Should Come — and Who Shouldn't</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Come if</strong> you want a genuine parade, the visible start of the city's holiday season, and cold-weather atmosphere over warm-weather sightseeing — and you're comfortable building Thanksgiving Day around outdoor viewing, a dinner reservation, and one indoor attraction that's actually open.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Skip it, or shift your dates, if</strong> museum-hopping every day is the priority, you won't book a holiday dinner reservation ahead, or you'd rather avoid parade-week rates and crowds. In that case, the quieter first three weeks of November — covered in the <a href="/chicago-in-november/">Chicago in November guide</a> — or the <a href="/chicago-fall-bucket-list/">Chicago fall bucket list</a> are the better fit.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="faq">FAQ: Thanksgiving in Chicago</h2>

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Is it worth going to Chicago for Thanksgiving?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes, for the right traveler. Chicago runs its own Thanksgiving Day parade, and the Lights Festival is scheduled just days before it. The trade-off is that Thanksgiving Day is a partial shutdown day: the Art Institute, Griffin Museum of Science and Industry, and Chicago History Museum close. Lincoln Park Zoo remains open, while ZooLights uses a separate evening calendar. Come for the parade and holiday atmosphere, but plan Thursday around confirmed openings.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Is the Chicago Thanksgiving Parade the same as the Macy's Parade in New York?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>No — separate events in separate cities. Chicago's parade, historically known as the McDonald's Thanksgiving Parade and now branded the Chicago Thanksgiving Parade, runs its 92nd annual edition on State Street from Ida B. Wells Drive to Randolph Street, 8 a.m. to 11 a.m. on November 26, 2026. It's smaller than Macy's, but a genuine, decades-old local tradition.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>What's open in Chicago on Thanksgiving Day?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Willis Tower Skydeck and Lincoln Park Zoo operate 365 days a year. The Chicago Park District lists the Maggie Daley Ice Skating Ribbon, Garfield Park Conservatory, and Lincoln Park Conservatory as open on Thanksgiving Day in 2026. The skating ribbon uses special holiday hours and requires reservations. The Art Institute, Griffin Museum of Science and Industry, and Chicago History Museum close. Restaurants use individual holiday schedules, so reserve and confirm directly.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Is Willis Tower Skydeck open on Thanksgiving?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes. The Skydeck operates every day of the year, including Thanksgiving, making it one of the few ticketed indoor attractions running on a day when most museums are closed.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>What's the weather like in Chicago for Thanksgiving?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Cold. National Weather Service climate normals for November 26 are about 43°F for the high and 30°F for the low. Wind can make exposed areas feel colder. Snow is possible but not guaranteed, so pack for cold and wind rather than a specific snow scene.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Are hotel rates higher during Thanksgiving week in Chicago?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Not always. Chicago hotel prices are dynamic, and Thanksgiving week can vary by property, neighborhood, booking lead time, and cancellation terms. Compare the same exact dates and final totals across a few central areas instead of assuming the holiday automatically means a higher or lower rate.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Is the Magnificent Mile Lights Festival on Thanksgiving Day?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>No. The Lights Festival is set for Saturday, November 21, 2026 — the Saturday before Thanksgiving, not the same day or the weekend after. Decorations stay up well past that night, so a Thanksgiving-week visit still catches the full Michigan Avenue display.</p>
