<p>New York on Thanksgiving Day is really two different cities layered on top of each other. Half of it shuts down completely — offices, most museums, most stores, Broadway — while the other half puts on the biggest, most crowded show of its entire year right through the middle of Manhattan. Knowing which half you're walking into changes everything about whether the trip is worth it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This guide covers exactly what's open and what's closed on Thanksgiving Day 2026 (November 26), what's actually worth doing besides the parade, and how to handle Black Friday if you're staying through the weekend.</p>

<h2 id="whats-open">What's Actually Open on Thanksgiving Day</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The big observation decks stay open: the Empire State Building, One World Observatory, and Top of the Rock all run on Thanksgiving Day. The Statue of Liberty and Ellis Island ferries operate as well, so the classic harbor visit is still on the table.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The city's zoos and aquarium stay open too — Central Park Zoo, Prospect Park Zoo, Queens Zoo, and the New York Aquarium are all running, which makes for a good lower-key option if you're traveling with kids and want something calmer than the parade crowds. A few smaller museums stay open as well, including the National Museum of the American Indian downtown.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Rockefeller Center's ice rink runs timed sessions on Thanksgiving Day, and Bryant Park's Winter Village — more than 180 shopping and food kiosks around a seasonal ice rink — is open too, so both of the city's signature holiday-season spots are in play even on the holiday itself.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Subways and buses run on Thanksgiving Day, but on a reduced Sunday schedule — build extra time into any transit-dependent plan, especially getting to or from the parade route.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 id="whats-closed">What's Closed</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Most major museums close for the day, including MoMA, the American Museum of Natural History, and the Met. Broadway goes dark on Thanksgiving Day as well — there are no evening performances, so don't plan a show around this specific date. Most offices, banks, and a large share of retail stores are closed too, particularly outside the main tourist corridors.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Most restaurants close for the day as well, though this is the one category with real exceptions — see the eating section below for what actually stays open.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 id="beyond-parade">Beyond the Parade: What's Actually Worth Doing</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If you're only in town for the parade itself, see my separate <a href="/macys-thanksgiving-day-parade-2026/">Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade guide</a> for the route, viewing spots, and the balloon inflation the night before. For everything else the day has to offer:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Fifth Avenue's holiday window displays</strong> go up before Thanksgiving and are worth the walk regardless of the parade — Saks Fifth Avenue's light-and-music show and Macy's animated windows are the two standouts. See them after dark, around 4:30pm, and aim for late afternoon or early evening on Thanksgiving Day itself, when the crowds are noticeably thinner than a weekend.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Rockefeller Center's ice rink</strong> is a straightforward way to spend an hour once the parade crowds thin out, especially since it's a short walk from the Fifth Avenue windows. Book a timed session rather than showing up and hoping.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Bryant Park's Winter Village</strong> gives you a market atmosphere without needing the parade at all — food kiosks, shopping, and its own ice rink with a rinkside restaurant if you want to sit down and warm up.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 id="eating">Where to Eat on Thanksgiving Day</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Don't assume you'll find a table by walking in. The restaurants that do stay open on Thanksgiving mostly run a set, prix-fixe holiday menu and take reservations that fill up well in advance — book as early as you can once your travel dates are set.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If a traditional turkey dinner isn't the goal, plenty of international restaurants stay open as normal since Thanksgiving isn't part of their calendar — Chinatown and the East Village are reliable neighborhoods to check for a non-traditional meal that doesn't require a holiday reservation months out.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 id="black-friday">Black Friday in NYC</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If your trip stretches into Friday, the city flips back to normal fast — stores reopen, restaurants reopen, and Fifth Avenue and Herald Square get genuinely crowded with shoppers on top of the usual tourist traffic. If shopping isn't the plan, treat Friday as a good day to do the museums that were closed on Thursday, since Black Friday crowds concentrate around retail, not necessarily around every museum.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->

<h2 id="faq">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Is Thanksgiving in NYC worth it?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes, if the parade and holiday atmosphere are what you're after. Most museums, offices, and many restaurants close, but observation decks, the Statue of Liberty ferry, city zoos, Rockefeller Center's rink, and Bryant Park's Winter Village all stay open, so there's a real day's worth of things to do beyond the parade itself.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Are museums open on Thanksgiving Day in NYC?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Most major museums are closed, including MoMA, the American Museum of Natural History, and the Met. A few smaller institutions, like the National Museum of the American Indian, stay open, along with the city's zoos and aquarium.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Do subways run on Thanksgiving in NYC?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes, subways and buses operate on Thanksgiving Day, but on a reduced Sunday schedule rather than a normal weekday timetable. Build in extra time for transit, especially around the parade route.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Can I find a Thanksgiving dinner in NYC without planning ahead?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It's risky. Restaurants that stay open on Thanksgiving mostly offer a set holiday menu with reservations that fill up well in advance. If you want a traditional dinner, book as early as possible. For a non-traditional option without a holiday reservation, check international restaurants in neighborhoods like Chinatown or the East Village, which mostly stay open as normal.</p>
