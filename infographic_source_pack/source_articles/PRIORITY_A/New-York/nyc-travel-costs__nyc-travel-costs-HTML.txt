<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>New York is expensive — but the way it's expensive is specific. The hotel room costs more than almost anywhere else in the US. The Broadway ticket is a real line item. But the coffee is $3, the pizza slice is $5, the subway costs the same whether you go two stops or twenty, and the best experiences in the city — the Brooklyn Bridge at dawn, Central Park, the High Line, the Staten Island Ferry — cost nothing at all.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>What inflates a New York budget is not the city itself. It's eating every meal near Times Square, taking taxis instead of the subway, and paying full price for observation decks that sell timed slots at a premium for walk-ups. The travelers who leave thinking NYC was extraordinarily expensive usually made those three mistakes every day for a week.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This guide breaks down exactly what New York costs in 2026 — accommodation, food, transport, sightseeing, and the things worth paying for — so you can plan an honest budget before you book your flights.</p>

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="accommodation">Accommodation Costs in NYC</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>New York hotel prices are among the highest in the US, and they fluctuate more than almost any other major city. Summer (June–August) and the Christmas window (mid-November through New Year's) are peak season — expect 20–30% higher rates than the same hotel in October. The total cost of a hotel in New York usually also includes a city occupancy tax of around 14.75% plus fees, so the checkout price is meaningfully higher than the room rate advertised.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Budget: $130–170/night</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Budget accommodation in New York means either a basic hotel in a less central location, a capsule or pod hotel, or a well-reviewed hostel. For a first trip with a full sightseeing schedule, location matters — a slightly higher room rate in Midtown or Brooklyn saves meaningful time and transit costs every day.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.booking.com/hotel/us/pod-times-square.html" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer"><strong>Pod Times Square</strong></a> is the hotel I would shortlist in this tier for Midtown. Compact rooms, but the location gives you walking access to Central Park, Top of the Rock, the High Line, and Times Square without a single subway ride. Strong for travelers who plan to be out all day and return late. Check the total room rate including taxes before comparing — the stated nightly rate can look lower than the checkout total.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Mid-range: $200–320/night</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The mid-range tier is where most first-time visitors to New York settle, and it covers a wide range of quality. Hell's Kitchen (the blocks between Eighth and Tenth Avenues in the 40s and 50s) is the best value zone in Midtown — genuinely walkable to Central Park and the major Midtown sights, less expensive than the East Side, and with some of the best independent restaurants in Manhattan one block away. Brooklyn (DUMBO, Williamsburg) can be meaningfully cheaper for the same quality level, with the L or A train connecting you to Manhattan in 20–30 minutes.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.booking.com/hotel/us/pod-brooklyn.html" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer"><strong>Pod Brooklyn</strong></a> in Williamsburg is the option I would shortlist in this tier if neighbourhood atmosphere and a real Brooklyn base matter more than walking to Midtown. L train to Union Square in 12 minutes; East River waterfront two blocks away. Before booking either hotel, compare total price including taxes, check recent reviews on room size and noise, and confirm cancellation terms.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For a comprehensive breakdown of all six NYC neighbourhoods — with price comparisons, honest pros and cons, and specific hotel shortlists for each area — see our <a href="https://milesandflavors.com/where-to-stay-in-nyc/">where to stay in NYC guide</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Comfortable: $350–600+/night</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The comfortable tier in New York delivers a significant step up in room size, lobby quality, and amenity access — the trade-off is that the base rate is higher to begin with, and taxes and fees push the total further. Properties in this tier in Midtown or the Upper East Side tend to include proper concierge service, gym access, and rooms large enough to feel like a genuine retreat from the city rather than just a bed. If a large room or a good bar matters to you, this is the tier where New York starts to feel genuinely luxurious rather than merely adequate.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="airport-transport">Getting to NYC from the Airport</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>New York is served by three airports, and each one has a different cost and time profile for the journey into the city. The right choice depends on your arrival airport, the time of day, and how much luggage you are carrying.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">From JFK International Airport</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>AirTrain + subway: $12.25 total.</strong> AirTrain from any terminal to Jamaica or Howard Beach station ($9.25), then the subway ($3) to Manhattan. Journey time: 50–75 minutes depending on destination. The cheapest option and reliable outside of rush hour.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Yellow cab: $70–75 flat rate</strong> from JFK to anywhere in Manhattan, plus tolls ($9–16 depending on route) and tip (20% recommended). Total: $88–108 for most Manhattan destinations. Journey time: 40–75 minutes depending on traffic.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Uber/Lyft: $55–90</strong> depending on time of day and surge pricing. Similar journey time to yellow cab. Worth comparing to the flat-rate taxi — at peak times taxi can be cheaper and more predictable.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">From Newark Liberty International (EWR)</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>AirTrain + NJ Transit: $16–19</strong> to Penn Station Manhattan. Journey time: 45–60 minutes. Good value if you are staying in Midtown or easily reach Penn Station.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Taxi/Uber: $65–100+</strong> with the Lincoln Tunnel toll and tip. Newark often has cheaper flights than JFK — the transfer cost difference ($40–60 more by taxi vs JFK) is usually offset by the airfare saving.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">From LaGuardia (LGA)</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>M60 bus: $3.</strong> Connects to the subway at 125th Street (A/B/C/D) and Astoria Boulevard (N/W). Journey time: 45–70 minutes to Midtown. Slow but the cheapest option.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Uber/Lyft: $25–50</strong> to Midtown. LaGuardia is the closest airport geographically, but traffic can extend this significantly during rush hour.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="getting-around">Getting Around the City</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The New York subway is one of the best-value transit systems for tourists in any major world city — $3 per trip regardless of distance, running 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, covering virtually every point on this list. OMNY (the city's tap-to-pay system) lets you use your contactless credit card or phone directly on the reader at every turnstile — no MetroCard, no fare card to buy, no tourist pass needed. As of 2026, a weekly OMNY cap means you pay a maximum of around $34 for unlimited rides in a 7-day rolling window using the same card or device.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Daily transport cost estimates:</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Subway-only day:</strong> $6–12 (2–4 trips — most sightseeing days in Manhattan involve more walking than subway rides)</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Subway + Citi Bike day:</strong> $15–20 (Citi Bike single-ride $4.49 or day pass $19 for unlimited 30-minute rides — good for Central Park and the High Line)</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Subway + occasional Uber day:</strong> $25–40 (for late nights, airport-area trips, or Brooklyn-to-Manhattan with luggage)</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Taxi/Uber-heavy day:</strong> $60–100+ (if you're skipping the subway entirely)</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>The rule in New York:</strong> use the subway for everything you can. A taxi or Uber between Midtown and Lower Manhattan costs $25–40 in moderate traffic; the same journey by subway is $3 and takes 20 minutes. The subway is not slower for most Manhattan trips — it often gets you there faster than a car, especially during daytime.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="food-costs">Food and Drink Costs in NYC</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>New York's food scene has two tiers that don't track with what you'd expect. The street-level, counter-service, and neighbourhood-restaurant version of New York is genuinely affordable — some of the best food in the city costs under $20. The tourist-facing version, especially within three blocks of Times Square or near the major Midtown hotels, is markedly worse and noticeably more expensive. The traveler who eats one block off the tourist circuit will pay less and eat significantly better.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Breakfast</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Bagel with lox and cream cheese: $12–18</strong> at a proper bagel shop (Ess-a-Bagel, Russ &amp; Daughters, Murray's Bagels). This is the essential New York breakfast — do it at least once.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Coffee + pastry: $8–12</strong> at a neighbourhood café</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Deli breakfast sandwich: $6–10</strong> — egg on a roll from a corner deli is a completely legitimate $7 breakfast eaten on the street</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Lunch</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Pizza slice: $4–6</strong> at a proper NYC pizzeria (Joe's, Di Fara, Prince Street). One of the best meals in the city and one of the cheapest.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Xi'an Famous Foods hand-pulled noodles: $13–16</strong> — multiple Manhattan locations, no reservations, consistently one of the best-value lunches in the city</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Chinatown: $10–18 per person</strong> for a full meal (soup dumplings at Joe's Shanghai, scallion pancakes from a street cart, dim sum on weekend mornings)</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Deli sandwich: $12–18</strong> at a classic NYC deli — a good option for eating while you walk between sights</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Dinner</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Neighbourhood restaurant (West Village, Williamsburg, Chinatown): $25–45 per person</strong> including a drink. This is the most honest representation of what good New York dining costs.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Smorgasburg or food market: $20–35 per person</strong> — weekend option in Williamsburg (Saturday) or Prospect Park (Sunday), with 70+ vendors and consistently high quality</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Casual restaurant near tourist areas: $35–65 per person</strong> — higher price, frequently lower quality</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Destination restaurant (Lilia, Via Carota, Russ &amp; Daughters Café): $55–90 per person</strong> — worth it if you plan one or two special meals around the rest of the budget</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Drinks</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Coffee: $3–6</strong> at a standard café; $6–8 at specialty spots</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Beer at a bar: $8–12</strong> in Manhattan; $7–10 in Brooklyn</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Cocktail at a rooftop bar: $18–26</strong> — arrive before 6pm to avoid the worst of the crowd premium</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Wine at a restaurant: $14–18 per glass</strong> in most mid-range Manhattan restaurants</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Tipping in New York:</strong> 20% at sit-down restaurants is the baseline — not optional in New York. 18% is considered low. Counter service and coffee shops: $1–2 per drink is standard. Tipping below 18% at a sit-down restaurant is noticed and remembered, especially in a neighbourhood spot. Budget this into your food figures.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="tours">The Tours Worth Paying For</h2>

<p>Most of New York's sights work fine independently — you don't need a guide to walk the Brooklyn Bridge or visit Central Park. <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="money-saving-tips">Money-Saving Tips for NYC</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Use OMNY, not a tourist pass.</strong> The $34 weekly cap with OMNY (tap your contactless card directly) is almost always better value than a tourist subway pass unless you are riding more than 12 times in a day. The CityPASS is only worth the math if several included attractions were already on your list — run the numbers before buying.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Eat one block off the tourist trail.</strong> The food on the piazza directly below the attraction is reliably worse and more expensive than the same category of restaurant one block away. This rule holds everywhere in the city.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Take the Staten Island Ferry.</strong> Free, runs every 30 minutes, passes the Statue of Liberty close enough for a clear view. The full Ellis Island and Statue of Liberty ferry tour is worth doing if you have time and want to go on the island — but the view and harbour crossing are free.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Book observation decks in advance.</strong> Walk-up same-day pricing at Top of the Rock and One World Observatory is higher than advance booking, and popular sunset slots sell out — especially in summer. Book both when you book your hotel.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>TKTS for Broadway.</strong> The TKTS booth in Times Square sells same-day tickets at 20–50% off for that evening's performances. Lines form before opening — worth it for a spontaneous evening if you're flexible on the show.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Lunch over dinner for restaurant splurges.</strong> The same kitchen at Lilia or Via Carota charges less at lunch than dinner, and the food quality is identical. If you're planning one high-end meal, a long lunch is the better-value version.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Governors Island.</strong> The best free outdoor escape in the city — 172 acres of car-free park in New York Harbour, 10 minutes by ferry from Lower Manhattan, with the best unobstructed view of the downtown skyline. Barely visited by first-timers, which is exactly why it works.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="practical-info">Practical Money Info</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Currency.</strong> US dollars everywhere. New York is exceptionally card-friendly — even street food vendors and market stalls increasingly accept contactless. Carry a small amount of cash ($20–40) for the occasional cash-only spot (Corner Bistro in the West Village, some dim sum carts in Chinatown) and for tipping at bars where card minimums apply.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Conversion fees.</strong> If you are paying in USD from a non-US bank account, every transaction adds a foreign exchange markup — typically 2.5–3.5% on top of the mid-market rate. On a $2,000 trip, that is $50–70 in invisible fees. A <a href="https://wise.prf.hn/click/camref:1101l5L5pb/destination:https%3A%2F%2Fwise.com%2Fregister%23%2Femail" target="_blank" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Wise card</a> eliminates the markup entirely and lets you spend in USD at the real exchange rate with no hidden charges. Worth comparing before the trip — especially for a city where most things are paid by card.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>ATMs.</strong> Widely available throughout Manhattan and Brooklyn. Avoid fee-heavy ATMs at convenience stores and tourist spots near Times Square — they charge $3–5 per withdrawal on top of any fees from your home bank. Use bank-branded ATMs (Chase, Bank of America, Citibank) where possible.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Tipping.</strong> 20% at sit-down restaurants is the baseline in New York — not optional. 18% is considered low; under 15% is noticed and considered poor form. Counter service and coffee shops: $1–2 per item is standard. Yellow cabs: 20% on the metered fare. Add this to your food and transport estimates when planning the budget.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Connectivity.</strong> New York's subway runs underground and blocks mobile signal throughout most of Manhattan. Download offline maps (Google Maps, Apple Maps, or Citymapper) before going underground. If your home mobile plan does not include reliable US data at a reasonable rate, an <a href="https://airalo.pxf.io/k4J7VV" target="_blank" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Airalo US eSIM</a> can be installed before the flight and activated the moment you land — covers navigation, OMNY transit, Uber, and all the basics from minute one.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="faq">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="how-much-does-nyc-cost-per-day">How much does New York City cost per day?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For a realistic mid-range trip, budget $300–420 per person per day including accommodation (room shared between two). This covers a mid-range Midtown or Brooklyn hotel, two restaurant meals, one paid attraction, and subway travel. A budget-focused traveler who sticks to free sights, eats pizza and bagels, and uses the subway can manage $175–220 per person per day. A comfortable trip with good hotels, Broadway, and restaurant dinners runs $600–900+ per person per day.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="is-nyc-expensive">Is New York City expensive?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes — New York is one of the most expensive cities in the world by the measures that matter most to travelers: hotels, restaurants, and entertainment. But it is also a city where the best experiences skew free (the Brooklyn Bridge, Central Park, the High Line, the Staten Island Ferry), where $5 buys you one of the best slices of pizza you'll eat anywhere, and where the subway gets you from Midtown to Brooklyn for $3. The city rewards informed travelers more than almost anywhere else — the gap between a knowledgeable visitor's costs and an uninformed one's is larger here than in most destinations.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="how-much-spending-money-for-nyc">How much spending money do I need for 5 days in NYC?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For 5 days in New York, budget approximately $870–1,300 per person for a budget trip (including accommodation), $1,550–2,525 for a mid-range trip, or $3,000–5,400+ for a comfortable trip. These figures exclude international flights. Add $200–400 for a Broadway show if that's on the list — it's the single largest discretionary item in a New York trip budget.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="how-much-does-broadway-cost">How much does a Broadway show cost?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Broadway tickets range from $75 to $300+ depending on the show, seat, and day of the week. Tuesday through Thursday performances are consistently cheaper than weekends for the same show. For same-day discounts of 20–50%, the TKTS booth in Times Square is the most reliable option. The TodayTix app runs rush and lottery tickets for $30–50 on some shows. If you have a specific show in mind, book 2–3 weeks ahead for the best seats at mid-range prices.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="cheapest-way-jfk-to-manhattan">What is the cheapest way from JFK to Manhattan?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The AirTrain from JFK to Jamaica or Howard Beach station ($9.25), then the subway to Manhattan ($3) — total $12.25. Journey time is 50–75 minutes depending on your destination in Manhattan. A yellow cab from JFK to anywhere in Manhattan charges a flat rate of $70–75 plus tolls ($9–16) and tip, total typically $88–108. Uber runs $55–90 depending on surge pricing — compare to the flat-rate cab before assuming Uber is cheaper, especially at peak times.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
