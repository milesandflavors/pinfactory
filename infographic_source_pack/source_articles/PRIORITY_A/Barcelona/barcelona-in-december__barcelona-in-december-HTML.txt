<p>December in Barcelona doesn't feel like the freezing, gray month most of Europe gets. Daytime temperatures stay mild by any winter-city standard, and that single fact changes the whole calculation for a December trip — including whether Barcelona beats Madrid if you're choosing between the two for a winter break.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> Barcelona in December stays mild — official climate normals at Barcelona Airport put the average high near 58°F (14.3°C) and the average low near 42°F (5.7°C). <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="weather">Weather in Barcelona in December</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Barcelona Airport climate normals put December's average high around 58°F (14.3°C) and average low around 42°F (5.7°C) — cool, especially after dark, but a light-to-mid-weight coat covers most days. Rain is more likely than in the warmer months but rarely disruptive for a whole trip; layer for a chilly morning that can turn into a genuinely pleasant early afternoon.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Sunshine hours drop compared to autumn, and the sea is well past swimmable — this is a city-and-culture month, not a beach month. Barceloneta is still worth a walk, just not a towel.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="vs-madrid">Barcelona vs. Madrid for a Winter Trip</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This is the comparison most people are actually weighing when they search for a December Spain trip. Madrid sits inland at higher elevation, which gives it a more continental winter climate — colder overnight lows, a real chance of frost, and a drier but sharper cold than Barcelona's coastal version. Barcelona's Mediterranean position keeps both the daytime highs and the overnight lows noticeably milder.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If comfortable outdoor walking is a priority — wandering the Gothic Quarter, sitting outside even briefly, a market stroll without serious cold — Barcelona wins on climate alone. Madrid has its own strong case on culture (the Prado, the Royal Palace) and is worth its own trip, but as a pure weather comparison for December, Barcelona is the easier month to be outside in.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="with-kids">Is December Good for a Family Trip?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes, with a few adjustments. The mild weather means you're not fighting bundled-up kids and short outdoor tolerance the way you would in a genuinely cold Christmas city — a real advantage over Chicago or New York in December if you're traveling with young children. Fira de Santa Llúcia and the Sagrada Família market both give kids something to look at and touch rather than just walk past, and traditions like Caga Tió and the caganer tend to land well with children once explained.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The trade-off is shorter daylight and a few closed days around Christmas itself — plan indoor backup options (museums, an early dinner) for late afternoons once the light goes, and check which days your planned restaurants and attractions are actually open before building the schedule.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="crowds">Crowds and Prices</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>December splits into two very different halves. The weeks leading up to Christmas see moderate crowds — busier than November, but nowhere near summer peak — with hotel prices climbing as Christmas week approaches. Christmas Day and December 26 (Sant Esteve, a Catalan public holiday) are genuinely quiet, with many restaurants and shops closed; major sights like Sagrada Família and Park Güell see noticeably thinner lines on those two specific days if you can get tickets. Late December into New Year's swings back to busy and expensive as the Reis season builds.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
