<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Public transport to the villages:</strong> Not practical — buses exist but won't get you where you need to go</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Verdict:</strong> Rent a car. It's not close.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></div>
<!-- /wp:group -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="getting-to-milan">Getting to Milan from the US</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Milan has two main airports. Malpensa (MXP) handles the transatlantic routes — direct flights from New York, Chicago, Miami, and other major US cities, plus connections via European hubs. It's 30 miles (50 km) northwest of the city center, about 45 minutes by road and 50 minutes by the Malpensa Express train.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Linate (LIN) is the city airport — closer in, mostly short-haul European flights. If you're connecting from another European city, you may land here. For most US travelers doing a Dolomites road trip, Malpensa is the arrival point and the place to pick up the rental car.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>From major US cities, Milan flight prices vary heavily by origin, season, and how early you book. Shoulder season — April, May, September, October — is usually easier on both airfare and crowds than summer peak. Our <a href="/how-to-find-cheap-flights/">guide to finding cheap flights</a> covers the approach that works for European connections.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Milan is also worth more than a transit stop. If your routing allows a free stopover before continuing to the Dolomites, a night or two in the city is a good use of that time. Our <a href="/free-stopover-flights-in-2026/">free stopover guide</a> explains how to set this up on certain airlines and routings.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="rent-a-car">Option 1: Rent a Car at Malpensa and Drive (What We Did)</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This is the right move for almost everyone doing a Dolomites trip. Picking up the car at Malpensa means you leave the airport and drive north — no transfers, no waiting for trains, no figuring out how to get your luggage onto public transport. You're on the A8 toward the A22 within 20 minutes of the terminal.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Book the car before you arrive. Online rates are usually better than what you'll be quoted at the airport desk, and in peak season the better car categories — automatics, especially — go fast. I would compare Malpensa pickup prices on <a href="https://discovercars.tpm.lv/EHat8d9Q" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Discover Cars</a> before choosing an agency, then read the insurance and cross-border wording carefully.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A compact automatic works well for this trip. The motorway section from Milan to Bolzano is straightforward. The mountain roads inside the Dolomites — the passes, the valley roads, the tracks down to remote parking areas — are where a smaller car genuinely helps. An SUV is not necessary and is often harder to maneuver on the narrower stretches.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Reality check:</strong> Italian motorways (autostrada) charge tolls. Have a credit card ready at the toll booths — most accept cards, but it's worth knowing the cash option exists too. For the full Milan–Dolomites drive, treat tolls as a real budget line and check a current toll calculator if you need an exact number for your route.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>IDP note:</strong> US license holders should carry an International Driving Permit (IDP) alongside their US license when driving in Italy. Arrange it before you leave through AAA or another authorized issuer, and keep both documents with you while driving.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="the-drive">The Drive: Milan to the Dolomites</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":1514,"sizeSlug":"large","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/leonardo-mott-Er9oNykHfBI-unsplash-1024x576.jpg" alt="A22 motorway through the Adige Valley on the drive from Milan to the Dolomites" class="wp-image-1514"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">The A22 through the Adige valley is one of the better motorway drives in Europe.</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The drive north from Milan is worth knowing in advance so it doesn't surprise you. The first hour is flat and industrial — Milan's suburbs, the Po plain, the approach to Verona. Nothing remarkable. Then the road enters the Adige valley and the mountains begin.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>From Verona north, the A22 runs through one of the most scenic motorway corridors in Italy. The valley narrows, the peaks close in on both sides, and the drive through Trento and up to Bolzano is legitimately beautiful. By the time you exit the motorway and start climbing into the valleys — Val Gardena, Val di Fassa, or the road east toward Cortina — the scenery is already delivering.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Key distances from Milan Malpensa (MXP):</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>MXP → Bolzano: ~195 miles (315 km) — about 3.25–3.75 hours</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>MXP → Ortisei (Val Gardena): ~220 miles (355 km) — about 4–4.5 hours</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>MXP → Canazei (Val di Fassa): ~235 miles (380 km) — about 4.25–5 hours</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>MXP → Cortina d'Ampezzo: ~270 miles (435 km) — about 4.5–5.5 hours</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>These are motorway times in reasonable traffic. Add 20–30 minutes during peak summer weekends, and an extra 30–45 minutes once you're off the A22 and onto the mountain roads into the specific valleys. The valley roads are not slow — they're well-paved and properly signposted — but they're winding, and it takes longer than the straight-line distance suggests.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The map below shows every airport option, route waypoint, and Dolomites destination so you can see the full drive before you commit to a base.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:html -->

</div>
<!-- /wp:html -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Local tip:</strong> Fill up the tank before you leave the motorway. Fuel in the mountain valleys is available but the stations are fewer and the prices are slightly higher. The last major fuel stop on the A22 before heading into the valleys is at Bolzano.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="train-option">Option 2: Train to Bolzano, Then Car</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The train from Milan to Bolzano is a legitimate option for the first leg — fast, comfortable, and often good value if booked in advance. Trenitalia and Italo both run the route, roughly 3–4 hours depending on the train, with one-way fares typically between €15 and €40.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Regional trains are cheapest but slowest, Frecciarossa high-speed is fastest but pricier — and either way, prices climb the closer you book to your travel date. Compare routes and prices on <a href="https://omio.sjv.io/c/7368997/3892654/7385" target="_blank" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Omio</a>. The train can be less tiring than driving the Milan–Bolzano section, especially if you want a night or two in Milan first.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The catch: once you're in Bolzano, you still need a car. The villages in the valleys above Bolzano are accessible by bus in limited ways, but you cannot do a proper Dolomites trip — reaching the passes, hiking from remote trailheads, getting to Tre Cime or Passo Pordoi — without your own transport. You can rent a car in Bolzano city center rather than at MXP; the selection is similar and the prices are comparable.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This combination makes most sense if you want a proper day or two in Milan before the mountain section. Take the train from Milan Centrale to Bolzano, pick up the car there, and start the mountain leg fresh. If you're going straight from the airport to the Dolomites, the train adds a transfer step that the car rental doesn't.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="verona-option">The Verona Option</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Verona Villafranca Airport (VRN) is worth considering if your routing goes through a hub that connects there — Munich, Frankfurt, London, or Amsterdam, for example. It's a smaller airport with fewer direct options from the US, but if you're connecting through Europe anyway, it puts you 1.5 hours from Bolzano instead of 2.5–3 hours from Milan.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Car rental is available at the airport. For the eastern Dolomites — Cortina, the Tre Cime area — Verona cuts the drive time more meaningfully than it does for Val Gardena or Val di Fassa.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="milan-stopover">Milan as a Stopover Before the Dolomites</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":1443,"sizeSlug":"large","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/ouael-ben-salah-0xe2FGo7Vc0-unsplash-1024x767.jpg" alt="Milan city view for an overnight stop before a Dolomites road trip" class="wp-image-1443"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Milan is worth more than a connection. One night minimum, two if the itinerary allows.</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We'd advocate for at least one night in Milan before heading into the mountains. The city is easy, has excellent food, and after a transatlantic flight the idea of immediately driving 3.5 hours on unfamiliar roads is less appealing than it sounds when you're planning from home.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Sleep in Milan, pick up the car in the morning, and arrive at your Dolomites base in the afternoon with energy left.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Milan has enough to fill two days — the Duomo, the Brera neighborhood, Navigli, the fashion district if that's your thing — and it's a genuinely good city for food. But even just one night, a walk around the center, and a proper dinner is better than treating it as a pure transit point.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For the hotel in Milan, the sweet spot is a 4-star in a walkable neighborhood close to the center. Porta Venezia, Brera, and the area around Corso Buenos Aires all work well — <a href="https://expedia.tpm.lv/hZNgMYvW" target="_blank" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">NH Milano Corso Buenos Aires</a> is the one I'd shortlist, because the travel day before a road trip has a way of changing its shape and a refundable rate is worth having.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="why-we-rented">Why We Rented a Car</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Dolomites don't have a public transport network that gives you access to the actual experience of the mountains. Buses run between the main towns. A few cable cars reach high viewpoints from those towns. But the thing that makes the Dolomites extraordinary — the high passes, the small mountain lakes, the roads that run along cliff edges with nothing below them, the ability to stop at a random viewpoint because the light is doing something remarkable — none of that is accessible without a car.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We knew this before we left, so the car rental wasn't a question. The question was where to pick it up. Malpensa made sense because it removed all transfers and let us start driving immediately after landing. Four hours after touchdown we were in the mountains.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The car also made the trip cheaper than it would have been otherwise. When you can drive between villages, you're not paying taxi rates or bus-pass limitations.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You eat where you want, stop when you want, and change plans when the weather changes. In the Dolomites, where the weather is highly local and a cloudy morning can become a perfect afternoon, that flexibility is the difference between a good trip and a great one.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="faq">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>How long is the drive from Milan to the Dolomites?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph {"placeholder":"Type / to add a hidden block"} -->
<p>From Milan Malpensa Airport: about 4–4.5 hours to Ortisei or Val Gardena, 4.5–5.5 hours to Cortina d'Ampezzo. From Milan city center, add 30–45 minutes. The motorway section (A8 to A4 to A22) is fast; the valley roads inside the Dolomites add time depending on your specific destination. Budget 30–45 minutes more than the GPS estimate on mountain roads.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Do you need a car to get from Milan to the Dolomites?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph {"placeholder":"Type / to add a hidden block"} -->
<p>Yes — for any meaningful Dolomites experience. The train gets you to Bolzano, from where buses serve the main valley towns. But the highlights — high mountain passes, remote trailheads, Lago di Braies, Tre Cime — are inaccessible without a car. Even travelers who prefer public transport for city trips consistently find that the Dolomites require a rental car. Pick it up at Malpensa Airport and you're on the motorway north within minutes.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Is it worth stopping in Milan before the Dolomites?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph {"placeholder":"Type / to add a hidden block"} -->
<p>Yes — strongly recommended for at least one night. Arriving after a transatlantic flight and immediately driving 3.5 hours on unfamiliar mountain roads is a recipe for a stressful first day. One night in Milan lets you recover, see the city (Duomo, Brera, Navigli — all genuinely worth your time), and start the mountain section refreshed. If the itinerary allows two nights, even better.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>What is the best airport to fly into for the Dolomites?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph {"placeholder":"Type / to add a hidden block"} -->
<p>Milan Malpensa (MXP) for most US travelers — it has the most transatlantic connections and direct routes from major US cities. Verona Villafranca (VRN) is worth considering if your European routing connects there; it's 1.5 hours from Bolzano versus 2.5–3 hours from Malpensa. Venice Marco Polo (VCE) works well if you're combining Venice and the Dolomites in one trip — the eastern Dolomites (Cortina, Tre Cime) are about 2–2.5 hours from Venice airport.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>How much does it cost to drive from Milan to the Dolomites?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph {"placeholder":"Type / to add a hidden block"} -->
<p>Budget for motorway tolls, fuel, parking, and the rental itself. Fuel and car rental costs vary too much by season and vehicle type to quote as fixed numbers, so compare your exact dates on <a href="https://discovercars.tpm.lv/EHat8d9Q" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Discover Cars</a> and add tolls as a separate line item.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Can you take a train from Milan to the Dolomites?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph {"placeholder":"Type / to add a hidden block"} -->
<p>Yes, as far as Bolzano — the train doesn't reach the mountain valleys themselves. Trenitalia and Italo run Milan Centrale to Bolzano in roughly 3–4 hours, with one-way fares typically €15–40 depending on the train type and how far ahead you book. From Bolzano you still need a rental car; the passes, lakes, and trailheads inside the Dolomites aren't reachable by public transport.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Are the Dolomites closer to Milan or Venice?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph {"placeholder":"Type / to add a hidden block"} -->
<p>It depends which part of the Dolomites you mean. For the western Dolomites — Val Gardena, Alpe di Siusi, Ortisei — Milan Malpensa is the better gateway, about 4–4.5 hours by car. For the eastern Dolomites — Cortina d'Ampezzo, Tre Cime di Lavaredo, Lago di Braies — Venice is significantly closer, around 2 hours by car, which is why it works as a (long) day trip from Venice but not realistically from Milan. See our <a href="/day-trip-to-the-dolomites-from-venice/">day trip to the Dolomites from Venice</a> guide if that's your starting point instead.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Is it possible to do a day trip to the Dolomites from Milan?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph {"placeholder":"Type / to add a hidden block"} -->
<p>Technically, but we wouldn't recommend it. Milan Malpensa to even the closest Dolomites towns is 3.5+ hours each way — a day trip means 7+ hours of driving for a few hours in the mountains. Venice is the realistic day-trip base for the eastern Dolomites, about 2 hours each way. From Milan, plan on at least one overnight in the mountains rather than trying to do it in a single day.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->
