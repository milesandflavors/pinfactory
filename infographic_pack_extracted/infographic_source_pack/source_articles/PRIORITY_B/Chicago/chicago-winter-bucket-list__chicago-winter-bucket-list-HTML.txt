<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="polar-plunge">The Chicago Polar Plunge</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This is the one that sounds like a joke until you're standing on North Avenue Beach watching thousands of people actually run into Lake Michigan in early March. The Chicago Polar Plunge benefits Special Olympics Illinois and Special Children's Charities, and recent editions have drawn more than 5,000 plungers to the beach for one genuinely unhinged, genuinely fun morning.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You don't need to swim to enjoy it — plenty of people show up purely to watch and cheer from the sand. If you do want to jump in, registration requires a minimum fundraising commitment (recent years have asked for around $200 for adults, $100 for plungers 17 and under), and on-site registration is usually available too, though advance sign-up is strongly encouraged since spots and timed waves fill up.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Price:</strong> Free to watch; minimum fundraising commitment to plunge, check current amount | <strong>Best time:</strong> Early March, North Avenue Beach | <strong>Time needed:</strong> 2-3 hours including travel | <strong>Tip:</strong> Bring a towel, dry clothes, and something warm to change into immediately — the actual swim lasts seconds, but the walk back to your bag doesn't.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="restaurant-week">Chicago Restaurant Week</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This is the pick I'd send a first-timer to over another rink stop. Chicago Restaurant Week runs for about two and a half weeks from late January into February, with hundreds of restaurants — recent editions have topped 550 — offering prix-fixe brunch, lunch, and dinner menus at set price points well below what those same kitchens usually charge.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It's the single easiest way to try restaurants that are normally hard to get into or expensive to justify on a short trip, and reservations open ahead of the event and fill fast at the most popular spots. Confirm current dates and the participating list before you go, since the roster changes every year.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Price:</strong> Set menus, historically around $25-55 per person depending on the restaurant | <strong>Best time:</strong> Late January through mid-February | <strong>Time needed:</strong> One meal, or build a whole trip around several | <strong>Tip:</strong> Book the restaurants you actually want first — the popular ones fill their Restaurant Week slots within days of reservations opening.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="garfield-park">Garfield Park Conservatory, Not a Mall</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>When it's 15 degrees outside, the tourist move is a shopping mall. The better move is the Garfield Park Conservatory — a genuine indoor rainforest and desert under glass on the West Side, with palm trees, a fern room, and warm, humid air that feels like a small miracle in February. It's less crowded and honestly more interesting than the Lincoln Park Conservatory most visitors default to.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It's closed Mondays and Tuesdays, open Wednesday until 8pm and Thursday through Sunday until 5pm. Chicago residents get in free with proof of residence; out-of-town adults pay a modest admission, with discounts for students, seniors, and kids.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Price:</strong> Around $10 adult, $5 senior/student, free for Chicago residents and kids 0-6 | <strong>Best time:</strong> Wednesday evening, when it stays open until 8pm and crowds thin | <strong>Time needed:</strong> 1-1.5 hours | <strong>Tip:</strong> Reserve a timed entry in advance — it's free but recommended to guarantee a spot.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="lunar-new-year">The Chinatown Lunar New Year Parade</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This is the cultural event most visitors never realize is happening. Chicago's Chinatown throws a full Lunar New Year parade — dragon dancers, lion dancers, marching bands, and floats — marching north along Wentworth Avenue toward Cermak. It follows the lunar calendar, so the date moves between late January and early March depending on the year, but it's free, family-friendly, and a completely different side of the city's winter calendar.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Get there early for a spot along Wentworth — the sidewalks fill in fast once the marching bands are audible, and the viewing stand area near Cermak gets the thickest crowds.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Price:</strong> Free | <strong>Best time:</strong> Check the current year's date — it follows the lunar calendar, typically late January to early March | <strong>Time needed:</strong> 1-2 hours | <strong>Tip:</strong> Grab lunch in Chinatown before or after — the restaurants are part of the day, not just the parade.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="sky-rink">The Peninsula's Sky Rink</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If Millennium Park's rink sounds crowded and a little chaotic, the Peninsula Chicago's rooftop Sky Rink is the version most tourists don't even know exists. It's a small rink on the hotel's fourth-floor terrace with a real Chicago skyline view, a chalet serving hot drinks, and a noticeably calmer crowd than the ground-level options — plus it benefits local children's charities.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Admission itself is free; skate rental runs extra. It typically runs into mid-February and is weather-dependent, with hours that shift week to week, so it's worth a quick call before you make the trip specifically for it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Price:</strong> Free admission; skate rental around $20 adult, $10 child | <strong>Best time:</strong> Friday-Sunday, confirm current hours by phone | <strong>Time needed:</strong> 1 hour | <strong>Tip:</strong> No reservation needed — just walk in, but call ahead since hours are weather-dependent.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
