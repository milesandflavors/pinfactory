<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Times Square is one of the most visited places on earth and one of the most polarizing. First-timers arrive expecting a cinematic moment — the lights, the scale, the electricity from every movie ever set in New York. What they actually get is louder, more crowded, and more overwhelming than any film suggested. That gap between expectation and reality is why some people love it immediately and some spend twenty minutes there and leave irritated. The honest answer: Times Square is worth visiting exactly once, at night, if you know what you're actually there for.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="what-times-square-is">What Times Square Actually Is</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Times Square is the stretch of Broadway between 42nd and 47th Streets where it crosses 7th Avenue — a bowtie-shaped intersection at the heart of Midtown Manhattan. The name comes from the New York Times, which moved its headquarters to the area in 1904 and lobbied the city to rename the neighborhood. The paper has since moved, but the name stayed.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>What makes it Times Square is the signage. The LED and neon billboards cover every available surface, many of them four or five stories tall, cycling through advertisements at all hours. The city's zoning laws actually require that buildings maintain a minimum level of illumination — Times Square is one of the few places in the world where landlords are legally obligated to keep the lights on. Hundreds of thousands of people can pass through on a typical busy day, which is why it often feels less like a square and more like a permanent event.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Broadway theater district radiates out from here — 41 theaters within a few blocks, most of them between 41st and 54th Streets. That's the reason most people who spend more than 45 minutes in Times Square are doing something specific: waiting for a show, picking up TKTS discount tickets, or meeting someone.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="day-vs-night">Day vs Night: When to Go</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Go at night. This is the single most important piece of advice for Times Square, and most visitors get it wrong by going during the day first and leaving underwhelmed.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>During the day, Times Square is a very busy, very loud intersection with a lot of competing stimuli. The billboards are on, but they compete with daylight and don't dominate the way they do after dark. The crowds are maximum density and there's no reward that justifies the density — you're essentially standing in traffic surrounded by tourists.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After dark — ideally 9:00 PM or later — the same intersection transforms. The billboards take over the entire visual field. The neon reflects off the pavement and the glass towers above. The sheer scale of the light installation becomes legible in a way it isn't during daylight hours. It's still crowded, still loud, and still overwhelming — but now that's the point rather than a side effect.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Tuesday and Wednesday evenings are usually less packed than weekends. If you have flexibility, those are the best nights. Avoid New Year's Eve unless you're fully committed — the crowds arrive ten hours early and the ball drop itself is visible for about 90 seconds.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="what-to-do">What to Do in Times Square</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Stand at the TKTS red staircase.</strong> The red glass staircase in the middle of the square — at 47th Street and Broadway — is the best free elevated vantage point in Times Square. You're above street level, surrounded by the billboard panorama, with a clear sightline down Broadway in both directions. This is the photograph most people are actually trying to take, and standing here for ten minutes gives you the full experience more efficiently than walking the streets below.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Check TKTS for same-day Broadway tickets.</strong> The booth under the red staircase sells same-day tickets and some next-day matinee tickets to Broadway and Off-Broadway shows, often at discounts of up to 50%. The selection changes constantly, so check the TKTS app before walking over. Hours vary by day; at the time of this update, the Times Square booth opens at 3:00 PM on Monday, Tuesday and Friday, and at 11:00 AM on Wednesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday. If Broadway is on your list and you are flexible about the show, this is genuinely one of the best deals in New York.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Walk the full crossroads.</strong> Go south on Broadway from 47th to 42nd, then back north on 7th Avenue. The full loop takes about fifteen minutes and gives you the square from all angles. Look up more than you look at street level — the scale of the billboards only registers when you're not staring straight ahead.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>The tourist stores.</strong> M&amp;M's World, the Hershey's store, and the NBA Store are genuinely fun for about twenty minutes each. They're entirely absurd and very good at being what they are. Nobody needs a four-pound M&amp;M dispenser, but the stores are well done and worth a walk-through if you're in the area.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="what-to-skip">What to Skip</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>The restaurants directly on the square.</strong> The restaurants with giant neon signs and Times Square views are, almost without exception, tourist traps. Prices are high, quality is low, and the view you're paying for is the same one you just had for free. Hell's Kitchen — one block west — has some of the best restaurant density in New York at normal prices. Eat there instead.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>The costumed characters.</strong> The Elmos, Spider-Men, and superhero characters who pose for photos in Times Square are not official. They work for tips and some can be aggressive about collecting them after a photo. If someone in costume approaches you for a photo, you can simply decline. If you do take a photo, have a dollar ready — they expect it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Spending more than an hour.</strong> There is no reason to spend more than 45-60 minutes in Times Square unless you're waiting for a Broadway show or eating at a specific restaurant nearby. Everything Times Square offers can be experienced in one focused visit. The first twenty minutes are impressive. The next forty are diminishing returns.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="how-long-nearby">How Long to Spend — and What's Nearby</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Budget 45 minutes for Times Square itself. That's enough to walk the full crossroads, spend time on the TKTS staircase, check the TKTS booth, and wander into one of the stores if you want to. After that, you've seen it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>What makes the visit worthwhile is combining it with the surrounding neighborhood. <strong>Bryant Park</strong> is an eight-minute walk east — a small, beautifully maintained park with good café options and a very different energy from the square. <strong>5th Avenue</strong> is five minutes in the same direction, with the New York Public Library main branch (free, worth going inside) at 42nd Street. <strong>Hell's Kitchen</strong> is one block west of 8th Avenue, running from roughly 34th to 57th Street — dense with restaurants and bars that have nothing to do with tourists. The best meal near Times Square is almost always in Hell's Kitchen.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If Broadway is part of the plan, timing your Times Square visit for around 7:00 PM lets you check TKTS, walk the square, and still make an 8:00 PM curtain with time to spare. Most theaters are within a five-minute walk of the TKTS booth.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="practical-tips">Practical Tips</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list {"className":"wp-block-list"} -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Getting there:</strong> The 1, 2, 3, N, Q, R, W, and S lines all stop at Times Square–42nd St. It's one of the largest subway stations in the system and impossible to miss. From Midtown hotels, it's often walkable.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Best night:</strong> Tuesday or Wednesday. The square is meaningfully less crowded than weekends, and Broadway shows run on both nights.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>TKTS booth hours:</strong> hours can change, so check TDF before you go. At the time of this update, Times Square TKTS is listed as 3:00 PM–8:00 PM on Monday, Tuesday and Friday, and 11:00 AM–8:00 PM on Wednesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday. The TDF TKTS app shows live inventory.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Photography:</strong> The TKTS staircase at dusk is the classic shot. Get there just as the sky turns dark blue and the billboards are at full brightness — about 30 minutes after sunset is the sweet spot.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>New Year's Eve:</strong> If the ball drop is your specific goal, arrive in the afternoon for a reasonable spot and plan to stay in place for hours. Bags are checked and re-entry isn't allowed. For most visitors, watching the drop from a nearby rooftop bar or on TV is a better trade.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="faq">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Is Times Square free to visit?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes — Times Square is completely free to visit. It's a public intersection, open 24 hours, with no admission fee. The billboards, the TKTS staircase, and the streets themselves cost nothing. The only things you'd pay for in Times Square are food, tickets (Broadway via TKTS), or purchases in the stores.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>What is the best time to visit Times Square?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After dark — ideally between 9:00 PM and 11:00 PM. The LED billboards that define Times Square only dominate the visual field once the sun is down. During the day the square is just a crowded intersection; at night it becomes the sensory experience that gives it its reputation. Tuesday and Wednesday evenings are less crowded than weekends.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>How long should I spend at Times Square?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>45 minutes is enough for most visitors. That covers the TKTS staircase, a walk of the full crossroads, a check of the TKTS booth for Broadway tickets, and time for one of the stores if you want. After about an hour the crowds and noise become repetitive rather than impressive. Combine your visit with Bryant Park or Hell's Kitchen nearby to make the most of the trip to Midtown.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Is Times Square safe for tourists?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes — Times Square is one of the most heavily policed areas in New York City. There's a visible NYPD presence at all hours, the streets are extremely well lit, and the combination of tourist density and security makes it one of the safer parts of Manhattan to walk around at night. The main nuisances are the costumed characters (who charge for photos) and aggressive flyer distributors — both are easy to decline and ignore.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>What is the TKTS booth in Times Square?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The TKTS booth is a discount Broadway ticket outlet run by the Theatre Development Fund, located under the red glass staircase at 47th Street and Broadway. It sells same-day and next-day tickets to Broadway and off-Broadway shows at up to 50% off face value. Selection varies by night — popular long-running shows appear most often. The TDF TKTS app shows live inventory so you can check what's available before heading over. Hours vary by day; check TDF before you go.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
