<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Florida and Christmas sound like a contradiction: no snow, no frost, and palm trees instead of pines. What Florida does instead is go all-in on lights. A handful of towns turn their historic downtowns into festive evening destinations every winter.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Two places lead the shortlist. St. Augustine has a 33-year-old Nights of Lights tradition and more than three million lights across the nation's oldest city. Mount Dora is a smaller lakeside town that trades scale for a compact, lower-commitment evening near Orlando.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This researched comparison uses the official 2026 event schedules available at the time of writing. It explains what each town offers, what is still awaiting confirmation, and which one is worth the drive for your trip.</p>

<h2 id="st-augustine">St. Augustine's Nights of Lights: The Reigning Champion</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>St. Augustine bills itself as the nation's oldest city, and its holiday display uses the historic setting rather than trying to imitate a snowy Christmas town. For the 2026-27 season, <strong>Nights of Lights runs from November 21, 2026, through January 18, 2027</strong>. This is the 33rd year of the tradition.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>More than three million white lights cover the historic downtown, including the bayfront, the Plaza de la Constitución, and balconies on Spanish colonial-style buildings. National Geographic has previously named Nights of Lights among the world's top holiday light displays, but the setting is the stronger reason to go: the lights extend beyond one park or one ticketed attraction.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Admission is free. Light-Up! Night begins at 4:00 PM on November 21, and the official countdown and switch-on are scheduled for 6:30 PM in the Plaza de la Constitución. Opening night adds live entertainment and ceremony, but it is also one of the busiest times to visit. If crowd comfort matters more than the kickoff atmosphere, choose a later weeknight.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The scale is the point. This is not a single park or one decorated street, so allow at least two to three hours for an unhurried walk. Stay overnight if you want time for dinner, shops, and a second route without turning the evening into a checklist.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>St. Augustine normally provides free park-and-ride service on selected Nights of Lights dates. The detailed 2026-27 shuttle calendar is still being finalized, so check the <a href="https://www.visitstaugustine.com/event/park-and-ride-shuttle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">current Nights of Lights parking and shuttle information</a> before you drive downtown.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 id="mount-dora">Mount Dora: the Small-Town Alternative</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Mount Dora is the answer if St. Augustine sounds like more than you want to take on. The <a href="https://www.mountdora.gov/972/Special-Events" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">official 2026 city calendar</a> confirms Light Up Mount Dora for <strong>Saturday, November 21, 2026</strong>. The city has not yet published the full 2026-27 nightly lighting period, so do not plan a January visit around last season's dates without checking again.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>What Mount Dora gives up in scale, it makes up for in walkability. Donnelly Park, Sunset Park, shops, and restaurants sit within a compact downtown. Park once, walk for an hour or two, and add dinner or coffee without reorganizing a full Florida itinerary.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If you want a specific event rather than a general lights walk, the 2026 Christmas Walk is scheduled for December 4. The Christmas Parade and Lighted Boat Parade follow on December 5. Check the final city schedule before traveling because times and street closures can change.</p>

<h2 id="other-towns">A Few Others Worth Knowing About</h2>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>St. Augustine and Mount Dora are not the only useful choices. These 2026 alternatives make more sense when they already fit your Florida route:</p>

<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Celebration:</strong> <a href="https://www.visitorlando.com/event/now-snowing/20897/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Now Snowing</a> is scheduled nightly from November 28 through December 31, 2026. The free event uses artificial snowfall in Celebration Town Center and suits families already staying near Walt Disney World.</li>

<!-- wp:list-item -->

<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Clearwater:</strong> the 52nd Annual Holiday Lighted Boat Parade is scheduled for December 12, 2026, at 6:30 PM. It is the most route-specific choice here and works best if you are already on the Gulf Coast.</li>

<h2 id="which-one">St. Augustine vs Mount Dora: Which One Should You Actually Visit</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If you can only pick one, base the decision on the trip you are already taking:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list {"className":"wp-block-list"} -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Choose St. Augustine</strong> for the biggest display, a historic setting, and a Christmas weekend with enough daytime sights to justify an overnight stay.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Choose Mount Dora</strong> if you are based in Orlando and want a lower-effort evening with less driving and a compact downtown.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Choose Celebration</strong> for a family-focused evening near Disney, Winter Park for shopping and a street-party atmosphere, or Clearwater for a Gulf Coast boat parade.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Mount Dora is about 45 minutes from Orlando, depending on traffic. St. Augustine is roughly 1 hour 45 minutes to 2 hours from central Orlando and is easier to justify as its own overnight trip.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 id="where-to-stay">Where to Stay</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For St. Augustine, stay inside or at the edge of the historic district if your budget allows. Parking pressure is highest on weekends, and walking back after dinner is easier than moving the car again.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://expedia.tpm.lv/Fjy6Lbbw" target="_blank" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">AC Hotel St. Augustine Downtown on Expedia</a> is on the quieter San Sebastian side of the Downtown Historic District. Guests report about a 10-minute walk to the historic streets. Paid, limited self-parking is the main tradeoff, so include it when comparing the total price.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For Mount Dora, prioritize a downtown-adjacent property only if you plan to stay overnight. For most Orlando-based visitors, it works better as an evening trip, so I would not choose a hotel solely for the lights.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 id="before-you-go">What to Know Before You Go</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Budget:</strong> the public displays are free, but parking, food, lodging, and optional tours are not. Compare the full evening cost rather than treating “free lights” as a free trip.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Weather:</strong> Florida winter evenings can be mild, but a cold front can push temperatures into the 40s or 50s°F (about 4-15°C). Pack a light jacket and an extra layer, then check the short-range forecast before leaving. Do not assume every December night will feel warm.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Crowds:</strong> weekends and the nights closest to Christmas are the busiest. A Tuesday or Wednesday usually gives you more room, particularly on St. Augustine's narrow historic streets.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Parking:</strong> arrive before dusk, check the current St. Augustine park-and-ride schedule, and allow extra time on opening night. The Viator cart tour does not include parking, so you still need a plan for reaching its meeting point.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Accessibility:</strong> St. Augustine's brick streets and crowds can make a long walking route harder for some visitors. The specific electric-cart tour linked above currently states that it is not wheelchair accessible, so confirm suitability directly before booking.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
