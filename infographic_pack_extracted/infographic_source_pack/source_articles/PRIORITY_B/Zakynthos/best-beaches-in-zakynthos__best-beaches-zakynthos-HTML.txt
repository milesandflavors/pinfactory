<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="1-navagio-shipwreck-beach-the-one-everyone-comes-for">#1 Navagio (Shipwreck Beach) — The One Everyone Comes For</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You've seen the photo. The rusted wreck on impossibly white sand, enclosed by 200-meter limestone cliffs, surrounded by water so turquoise it looks retouched. The photo is accurate. Navagio really does look like that.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>What the photo doesn't show: in recent seasons, access has been heavily restricted because of rockfall risk from the cliffs above. Boats can usually stop very close to the shore, just a few meters from the beach, but they are not allowed to let passengers step onto the sand. In practice, you see the cove, the wreck, and the cliffs from the water, get your photos, and then leave. For most travelers, that's still enough. The view from the boat at close range is genuinely breathtaking.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The official clifftop viewpoint above Navagio can also be worth the drive, but treat it as a restricted viewpoint, not a place to wander around the cliff edge. Access rules have changed in recent years, and the wider cliff area is exactly where the landslide risk matters most. If the official viewing platform is open when you visit, it gives you a completely different angle from the boat trip.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>What to know:</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Boat/viewpoint only — departures usually run from Porto Vromi (west coast) or Agios Nikolaos (north)</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Boats can get close, but passengers cannot currently step onto the beach — verify the current rule before booking</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Use only the official viewpoint if it is open; do not follow informal cliff-edge paths</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Morning departures get better light and fewer boats at the site</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For the boat trip, book in advance because morning slots with smaller groups fill up fast in summer. I would compare <a href="https://www.viator.com/searchResults/all?text=Navagio+Blue+Caves+boat+tour+Zakynthos&amp;pid=P00300034&amp;mcid=42383&amp;medium=link&amp;campaign=best-beaches-in-zakynthos" target="_blank" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Navagio and Blue Caves boat tours on Viator</a>, then choose a morning departure that clearly explains the current access rules.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="2-gerakas-the-best-beach-you-can-actually-swim-at">#2 Gerakas — The Best Beach You Can Actually Swim At</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":1281,"sizeSlug":"large","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/IMG_1384-1024x768.jpg" alt="Gerakas Beach in Zakynthos with calm clear water and protected turtle nesting area" class="wp-image-1281"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Gerakas: no sunbeds, no bar, no crowds. Just one of the best beaches in Greece.</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Gerakas is at the southern tip of the Vassilikos peninsula, about 35 minutes from Zakynthos Town, and it's the best beach on the island that you can fully experience on foot. Wide, pale sand, clear calm water, and low cliffs at the edges — it looks like a postcard and feels like somewhere that hasn't been packaged yet.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That's by design. Gerakas is a protected loggerhead sea turtle nesting site, and Zakynthos is one of the most important loggerhead nesting areas in the Mediterranean. The nesting area is fenced off, and visitors are asked to stay close to the waterline and leave the beach at sunset. There are no beach bars or water sports on the sand. The National Marine Park rules are exactly what keep the beach feeling this good.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The result is a beach that feels like what beaches are supposed to feel like. The drive from town filters out everyone who isn't willing to make the effort, and the restrictions keep the experience intact.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Local tip:</strong> In late summer during hatching season, you may spot baby turtles making their way from the sand to the sea. The guides present on the beach will show you where to watch without disturbing the nests. It's one of those things that stays with you.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>What to know:</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>~35 minutes from Zakynthos Town</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Leave at sunset and follow the National Marine Park signs during nesting season</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>No facilities — bring water, snacks, and sun protection</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Respect the fenced nesting areas, especially in hatching season</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="3-xigia-sulfur-beach-strange-fascinating-unlike-anything-else">#3 Xigia (Sulfur Beach) — Strange, Fascinating, Unlike Anything Else</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":1319,"sizeSlug":"large","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/12photostory-HhlRkFf-UiA-unsplash-1024x683.jpg" alt="Xigia Sulfur Beach in Zakynthos with turquoise water and rocky coastline" class="wp-image-1319"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">The smell is real, the water is strange, and there's nowhere else on the island like it.</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Xigia is on the northeastern coast, about 22 km from Zakynthos Town, and technically it's a beach — but you don't go to Xigia for the beach. Natural sulfur springs bubble up from underwater karst caves directly into the sea, creating patches of warm, milky-white water mixed with the cooler turquoise around them. The smell is noticeable. The whole experience is genuinely strange.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Whether you love it or find it bizarre probably depends on your expectations. Go knowing you're there for a natural phenomenon and not a conventional beach day, and it's fascinating. The water is actually good for swimming — people come specifically for the supposed benefits of the sulfur water — but the real draw is how unusual it is. We arrived early in the morning and had the place largely to ourselves — on a Greek island in summer, that almost never happens.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Reality check:</strong> There are three small beaches in the Xigia area. The main Xigia beach has the strongest sulfur concentration and is the busiest. Earlier in the day is better — it can get crowded mid-morning in peak season.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>What to know:</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>~22 km northeast of Zakynthos Town</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>The sulfur smell is real — it won't bother everyone but it's distinctive</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Three beaches in the area; the main one is signposted</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Combine with Blue Caves for a good north-island day</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="4-blue-caves-not-a-beach-but-dont-skip-it">#4 Blue Caves — Not a Beach, But Don't Skip It</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":1322,"sizeSlug":"large","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/IMG_1354-edited.jpg" alt="Blue Caves boat trip in Zakynthos with clear blue water and limestone cliffs" class="wp-image-1322"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">The Blue Caves are best from the water — the color inside is something a camera barely captures.</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Blue Caves aren't a beach — they're sea caves cut into the limestone cliffs along the northern coast, just south of Cape Skinari. What they are is one of the most visually arresting things on the island: sunlight filtering through the cave openings turns the water inside an almost electric shade of blue-green that photographs don't quite do justice to.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You can swim here — and you should. The water inside the caves is some of the clearest we've seen anywhere. You can see straight down to the bottom, deep and blue, and jumping in from the boat is one of those things that feels completely different from reading about it. It's one of the highlights of the entire trip.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Small wooden boats from Agios Nikolaos take you directly into the caves — the trips are short (10–15 minutes from the harbor) and most operators combine them with a Navagio tour. The best light is between 9 and 11am. You can also drive to a clifftop viewpoint above the caves from the north of the island, which gives a good perspective over the coastline even if you're not doing the boat trip.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>What to know:</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Boat tours from Agios Nikolaos harbor (northern tip of the island)</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Best visited 9–11am for the light</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Most tours combine Blue Caves + Navagio — a good use of a half day</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Clifftop viewpoint accessible by car — worth stopping even without the boat</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="5-kalamaki-the-quiet-option-close-to-everything">#5 Kalamaki — The Quiet Option Close to Everything</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Kalamaki sits between Laganas and the quieter southern coast and is the beach to choose when you want a full day by the water without noise. Fine golden sand, calm sheltered water, and a pace that feels genuinely relaxed. It's close enough to Laganas that the infrastructure exists — a handful of tavernas, some sunbeds — but far enough that the crowd thins out considerably.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Kalamaki is also a sea turtle nesting site, like Gerakas. The nesting areas are marked and off-limits at night during nesting season, but the beach is open and good during the day. It's the kind of beach that doesn't have a dramatic story but delivers exactly what a beach should: a good day in the sun with calm water and no aggravation.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>What to know:</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>About 10 km from Zakynthos Town</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Night restrictions in nesting season (same as Gerakas)</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Some sunbeds and tavernas nearby</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Calmer end of the Laganas stretch — a good alternative if you're based in that area</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="6-tsilivi-the-most-convenient-beach-on-the-island">#6 Tsilivi — The Most Convenient Beach on the Island</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Tsilivi is on the east coast about 5 km from Zakynthos Town, and it's the island's main family resort beach. Shallow gradual water, wide sandy shore, sunbeds, watersports, a water park nearby — it has everything that a well-run resort beach is supposed to have. Nothing about Tsilivi will surprise you. That's the point.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If you're traveling with young children, Tsilivi makes good sense. If you want dramatic scenery or a remote experience, you'll want to drive further. As a convenient beach for a morning swim before heading somewhere more interesting in the afternoon, it works well. The location close to town means it's an easy stop rather than a destination.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>What to know:</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>5 km from Zakynthos Town — easy drive or short taxi</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Full amenities: sunbeds, watersports, restaurants nearby</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Gets crowded in peak summer, but the beach is wide</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Shallow water — good for children</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="7-laganas-the-longest-beach-you-know-what-it-is">#7 Laganas — The Longest Beach. You Know What It Is.</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Laganas is the longest beach on the island — 9 km of golden sand stretching toward Kalamaki. The water is clean, the beach is wide, and the sand is fine. It's also backed by the island's busiest resort strip, and from July to August it's as crowded as a popular Greek resort beach gets.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If you want sun loungers, bars within steps, watersports, and the full package — Laganas delivers. If you want any of the other things that make Zakynthos worth visiting, you'll need to get in a car and drive somewhere else. Laganas as a beach is fine. As the only beach you visit on the island, it would be a shame.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Reality check:</strong> The end of the beach closest to Kalamaki is noticeably calmer than the main Laganas strip. If you're in the area and want something quieter, walk toward Kalamaki — the beach continues and the crowd thins.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>What to know:</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>9 km long — island's longest beach</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Very crowded July–August</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Full resort infrastructure throughout</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Calmer toward the Kalamaki end</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="which-beach-is-right-for-you">Which Beach Is Right for You?</h2>
