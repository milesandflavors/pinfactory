The price difference in shoulder season is often just $30–50 per person per night — worth it. Per person per night at a good 4–5 star all-inclusive property:</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="transport">Transport</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Airport to resort:</strong> $15–30 by private taxi. Some resorts include this in the package — check before arranging separately. The drive from Marsa Alam airport (RMF) to the Coraya Bay cluster is about 20–25 minutes.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Local taxis:</strong> Always negotiate before getting in. Agree on the total price in Egyptian pounds before you move. The starting ask is typically 30–50% above the settled price — this is standard and expected. Stay calm, counter once, and you'll reach a fair number quickly.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="tipping-budget-for-it-properly">Tipping: Budget for It Properly</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Tipping isn't optional in Egypt — it's part of how the system works, and resort staff quality is directly connected to it. Budget honestly for this rather than treating it as an afterthought.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Room cleaning: $1–2 per day (leave daily, not at checkout)</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Restaurant servers at the resort: $1–2 per meal for attentive service</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Excursion guides: $5–10 per person for a half-day; $10–20 for a full day</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Dive instructors: $10–20 if the dive was good</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Taxi drivers: round up to the nearest convenient amount</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Budget roughly $60–90 per person for a 7-night trip. It's a real line item, and it makes a genuine difference to the people providing the service.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes — and more so than most people expect before they run the numbers. A 5-star all-inclusive on the Red Sea for $2,000–2,250 per person including flights from the US is genuinely competitive with 4-star beach options in Southern Europe at comparable price points. You get a higher room and service standard, all food and drink included, and one of the most impressive marine wildlife destinations in the world within swimming distance of your sun lounger. That combination is hard to beat at this price.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The all-inclusive format also removes a category of daily decision fatigue that's worth more than it shows up in a spreadsheet. No meal cost calculation, no food budget stress, no wondering whether the restaurant is worth it tonight. For a family trip, a low-effort beach week, or anyone who wants fewer daily decisions, that simplicity has real value.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="when-to-book-for-the-best-price">When to Book for the Best Price</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>October is the sweet spot — I'd book it without hesitation. Comfortable weather, significantly lower prices than December–January, noticeably fewer crowds at both the resort and the excursion sites. Three to four months ahead for the resort with free cancellation. For flights, track via <a href="https://www.google.com/flights" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer">Google Flights</a> 2–4 months out and move when you see a price you're comfortable with rather than waiting for a deal that may not arrive.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="how-much-does-a-week-in-marsa-alam-cost">How much does a week in Marsa Alam cost?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A realistic 7-night Marsa Alam trip from the US usually costs around $1,800-2,800 per person, including flights, a 4- or 5-star all-inclusive resort, a few excursions, tips, transfers, visa cost, and basic connectivity. The biggest variables are flight routing, resort tier, and whether you travel in shoulder season or peak winter.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="is-marsa-alam-expensive">Is Marsa Alam expensive?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Marsa Alam is not cheap once long-haul flights are included, but it can be strong value compared with many beach trips because resort stays are usually all-inclusive and the house reef can replace several paid activities. December and January are the expensive months; October and November are usually better value.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="do-you-need-travel-insurance-for-marsa-alam">Do you need travel insurance for Marsa Alam?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes, especially if you plan to snorkel, dive, take boat trips, or do desert excursions. Check that your policy covers Egypt, medical evacuation, water activities, and scuba diving if you are certified and plan to dive. <a href="https://ektatraveling.tpm.lv/fqijSuju" target="_blank" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">EKTA</a> is one option worth comparing before you book.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="what-currency-is-used-in-marsa-alam">What currency is used in Marsa Alam?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The local currency is the Egyptian pound. USD and euros may be accepted in tourist settings, but you usually get better control by paying in local currency or using a travel card with fair exchange rates. Keep small notes for tips and agree taxi prices before you get in.</p>
