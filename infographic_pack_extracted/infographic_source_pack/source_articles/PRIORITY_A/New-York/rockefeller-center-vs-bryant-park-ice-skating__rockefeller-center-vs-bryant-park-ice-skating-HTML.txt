<h2 id="rockefeller">Rockefeller Center: The Postcard Rink</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Rockefeller Center's rink is small — genuinely smaller than most people expect from photos — and it's timed, usually sold in roughly one-hour sessions with dynamic pricing that climbs sharply around evenings, weekends, and the holidays themselves. General admission runs from about $22 for off-peak slots up to $112 or more for VIP sessions, and skate rental adds roughly $12 on top if you don't bring your own.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>What you're paying for is the setting: the tree overhead, the gilded Prometheus statue, and the surrounding skyscrapers in every direction. It's genuinely one of the most photographed few square meters in the city, and if that specific scene is what you came for, no other rink replicates it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2 id="bryant-park">Bryant Park: The Actual Skate</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Rink at Bryant Park flips the model: admission is free if you bring your own skates, with no timed session to watch the clock on. If you need rentals, current pricing runs roughly $19 to $60 including fees, still generally cheaper than a comparable Rockefeller slot. The rink itself is noticeably bigger, which means more actual room to skate rather than shuffling around a crowded circle.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It's also part of the larger Bryant Park Winter Village, so skating pairs naturally with the Holiday Shops and food vendors around it — you can make a full afternoon of the same stop rather than skating and then leaving to find everything else.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 id="which-one">Which Rink Wins</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If you're choosing based on the photo alone, Rockefeller Center wins — nothing else looks like it. If you're choosing based on the actual experience of skating without rushing and without a steep bill, Bryant Park wins clearly. My honest suggestion for most first-timers: walk by Rockefeller Center for the view and the photo, even if you don't skate there, and save your actual skating time and budget for Bryant Park.</p>
