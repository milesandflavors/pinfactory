<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="budget-90-140-night">Budget: $90–140/night</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Chicago has a solid budget hotel scene concentrated in the downtown area and River North. Private rooms in budget hotels outside the immediate city center start around $80–90 on weekdays and can drop significantly in winter. For a well-located budget-friendly option with good reviews, citizenM Chicago Downtown consistently delivers quality at fair prices. <a href="https://www.expedia.com/Hotel-Search?destination=citizenM+Chicago+Downtown+Illinois" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Expedia →</a> or <a href="https://www.booking.com/hotel/us/citizenm-chicago-downtown.html" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Booking.com →</a></p>

<p>This is the sweet spot for most travelers — well-located hotels in the Loop, River North, or Magnificent Mile area with good amenities and reliable quality. Prices vary significantly by season and day of the week, with weekends often running $30–50 higher than weekdays. Loews Chicago Hotel is a consistently solid mid-range choice with a great River North location. <a href="https://www.expedia.com/Hotel-Search?destination=Loews+Chicago+Hotel+Illinois" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Expedia →</a> or <a href="https://www.booking.com/hotel/us/loews-chicago.html" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Booking.com →</a></p>

For a special occasion or a splurge trip, the quality and location justify the price. <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="food-drink-costs-in-chicago">Food &amp; Drink Costs in Chicago</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Chicago is one of America's great food cities — and the range of price points is genuinely impressive. You can eat extraordinarily well here without spending much, or you can blow the budget at some of the country's finest restaurants. The honest truth: the $6 hot dog and the $40 dinner are both worth having.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="street-food-casual-5-18">Street food / casual: $5–18</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Chicago-style hot dogs ($5–8), Italian beef sandwiches ($8–12), deep-dish pizza by the slice ($5–8), and tacos in Pilsen ($3–5 each) represent some of the best value eating in the city. Portillo's — a Chicago institution — does hot dogs and Italian beef sandwiches that most locals would put up against any restaurant in the city, and nothing on the menu tops $12.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="mid-range-restaurant-20-45-per-person">Mid-range restaurant: $20–45 per person</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A sit-down meal with drinks at a solid restaurant in River North or the West Loop will run $25–40 per person including tip. Chicago's restaurant scene is extraordinary at this price point — you're not compromising on quality. The West Loop's Restaurant Row is worth a dinner even on a moderate budget.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="fine-dining-80-200-per-person">Fine dining: $80–200+ per person</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The West Loop has some of the finest dining in America — Girl &amp; the Goat, The Publican, Avec, and dozens of others. Budget $80–120 for a serious dinner with wine at a top-tier restaurant. Alinea sits at the extreme end at $350+ per person — one of the best restaurants in the world, but clearly a special occasion choice.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="supermarket-self-catering">Supermarket / self-catering</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A Whole Foods and several Trader Joe's locations sit in the downtown area. Buying breakfast and lunch supplies from a supermarket and eating out only for dinner is one of the most effective ways to reduce Chicago food costs — you could save $30–50 per day compared to eating every meal out.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="must-try-chicago-foods-with-prices">Must-try Chicago foods with prices</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Chicago deep-dish pizza (Lou Malnati's, Giordano's): ~$25–30 per person</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Chicago-style hot dog (Portillo's): ~$5–7</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Italian beef sandwich: ~$8–12</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Chicago-style popcorn (Garrett Popcorn): ~$8–15 for a bag</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Tacos in Pilsen: ~$3–5 each</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> Avoid eating on the Magnificent Mile and directly around Navy Pier — these are the most tourist-priced areas in the city. Walk two blocks in any direction and prices drop noticeably. The West Loop, Wicker Park, and Pilsen all offer significantly better value.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="transport-costs-in-chicago">Transport Costs in Chicago</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="flights">Flights</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Chicago O'Hare (ORD) is one of the busiest airports in the world with excellent connections from across the US and internationally. Midway Airport (MDW) serves budget carriers like Southwest and often offers cheaper fares — worth checking both when planning. Cheapest months to fly in: January through March. Most expensive: summer (June–August) and major convention periods.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="local-transport">Local transport</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The CTA 'L' train covers all the major tourist areas at $2.50 per ride with a Ventra card. Day passes are the better value for most visitors:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>1-day pass: $5</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>3-day pass: $15</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>7-day pass: $20</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>CTA fares can change, so check the <a href="https://www.transitchicago.com/fares/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">official CTA fare page</a> before you travel, especially if you are building a tight budget around transit passes.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For a 3-day trip with multiple daily rides, the 3-day pass almost always works out cheaper than paying per ride. The Red and Blue lines run 24 hours — useful for late nights.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="taxi-uber-average-costs">Taxi / Uber average costs</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Airport (O'Hare) to downtown: ~$35–55</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Airport (Midway) to downtown: ~$25–35</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Short downtown trips: ~$10–15</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Cross-city trips (downtown to Wicker Park): ~$15–20</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Surge pricing during Friday and Saturday evenings can make these trips noticeably more expensive. The L train is almost always the better option for downtown travel.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="divvy-bike-share">Divvy bike share</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Day passes cost $15–18 and are excellent for the lakefront trail and shorter distances in good weather. Hundreds of docking stations throughout the central areas.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> Public transport + walking covers 95% of what first-time visitors need to see. Buy a 3-day pass on arrival if you expect several rides per day — it usually beats paying ride by ride.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Connectivity costs for international visitors.</strong> If you're traveling from outside the US, roaming charges on a European or Australian SIM can run $5–10 per day on top of everything else. A US eSIM from <a target="_blank" href="https://airalo.tpm.lv/TYjQRZua" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Airalo</a> typically costs $8–15 for a week of data — you install it before you fly and activate it on landing. No SIM swapping, no finding a phone store at O'Hare.</p>

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="is-chicago-worth-the-cost">Is Chicago Worth the Cost?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes — and more so than most major US cities. What makes Chicago particularly good value is the quality of its free offering. The lakefront, the parks, the Riverwalk, and Lincoln Park Zoo are world-class experiences that cost nothing. Add one or two paid attractions per day and you have a memorable trip without spending anywhere near New York or San Francisco levels.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The architecture boat tour is the one paid thing that's non-negotiable — $50 for 90 minutes that transforms how you understand the city. Everything else is a matter of priorities and budget.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="budget-mistakes-most-visitors-make">Budget Mistakes Most Visitors Make</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Paying per CTA ride instead of getting a 3-day pass — run the math before you tap your first journey. Three rides in a day and the pass already wins.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Booking accommodation without checking 6–8 weeks ahead. A convention can fill downtown hotels overnight and prices triple. Book early, with free cancellation.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Eating deep-dish at dinner — longer wait, same price, and you're too full to enjoy the evening after. Lunch is the better call.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Eating anywhere on the Magnificent Mile. Two blocks away is a completely different price world with better food.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="money-saving-tips-for-chicago">Money Saving Tips for Chicago</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="1-visit-in-shoulder-season">1. Visit in shoulder season</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>April–May and September–October offer the best balance of good weather and lower prices. Hotel rates are often lower than peak summer, flights can be cheaper, and major attractions are significantly less crowded. September is the top pick for a first visit.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="2-buy-the-city-pass-if-visiting-multiple-museums">2. Buy the CityPASS if visiting multiple museums</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If your itinerary includes the Skydeck, Shedd Aquarium, and Field Museum, CityPASS can save money compared with buying separate tickets, but only if you actually want the included attractions. Compare the pass against your exact plan before you buy. <a href="https://www.viator.com/tours/Chicago/Chicago-CityPass/d673-2640CHI_TR?pid=P00300034&amp;mcid=42383&amp;medium=link&amp;campaign=chicago-travel-costs" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Check CityPASS availability on Viator →</a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="3-book-accommodation-well-in-advance">3. Book accommodation well in advance</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Chicago's convention calendar fills downtown hotels rapidly and unpredictably. Booking 6–8 weeks ahead guarantees both availability and reasonable pricing. Last-minute booking in Chicago is expensive — this is not the city to wing it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="4-eat-where-the-locals-eat">4. Eat where the locals eat</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Magnificent Mile and Navy Pier have the most tourist-inflated prices in the city. The West Loop, Wicker Park, Pilsen, and Logan Square all offer significantly better food at better prices — and a more authentic Chicago experience.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="5-use-the-l-train-instead-of-uber">5. Use the L train instead of Uber</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A 3-day CTA pass costs $15 and covers unlimited rides. The equivalent in Uber fares for the same trips would easily run $60–80. The L train is also faster than Uber during peak hours. Use Uber for late-night returns and off-network trips only.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="6-visit-in-january-or-february-for-lowest-prices">6. Visit in January or February for lowest prices</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If cold weather does not bother you, winter is usually the cheapest time to visit Chicago by a significant margin. Hotels are often much cheaper than summer rates, and the city's indoor attractions — the Art Institute, the Field Museum, the Shedd — are just as good in February as in July.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="practical-money-info">Practical Money Info</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="payment-methods">Payment methods</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Cards are accepted virtually everywhere in Chicago — restaurants, taxis, street food vendors, and even some hot dog carts. Contactless payment (Apple Pay, Google Pay) works at most modern terminals. Cash is useful for tips and some smaller vendors but rarely essential.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="tipping-the-full-breakdown">Tipping — the full breakdown</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Tipping in Chicago — and across the US — is not optional. It's a cultural expectation and a significant part of service workers' income. Budget an additional 30–35% on top of menu prices to account for both tax and tip.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Restaurants:</strong> 18–20% standard, 15% absolute minimum for adequate service, 22–25% for excellent service</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Bars:</strong> $1–2 per drink at casual bars, $2–3 for cocktails</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Taxi/Uber:</strong> 15–20%</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Hotel housekeeping:</strong> $2–5 per night, left daily</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Tour guides:</strong> $5–10 per person for guided tours</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="travel-insurance-costs">Travel insurance costs</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>US medical costs are high, so international visitors should price travel medical cover before the trip. Look for emergency medical coverage, trip interruption or cancellation terms, baggage cover, and exclusions. <a href="https://ektatraveling.tpm.lv/fqijSuju" target="_blank" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">EKTA</a> is a useful starting point for travel medical cover, but always read the policy wording before buying.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="taxes">Taxes</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Chicago has one of the highest sales tax rates in the US — approximately 10.25% on most goods and services. This is not included in displayed prices and will be added at checkout. Restaurant bills will always be higher than the menu prices once tax and tip are added.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="currency-exchange-for-international-visitors">Currency exchange (for international visitors)</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Exchange currency before you arrive or use your bank card at an ATM on arrival — airport exchange desks typically offer rates 5–10% worse than bank rates. Use bank ATMs (Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo) over third-party ATMs in hotels and convenience stores, which charge $3–5 per withdrawal. The easiest option for international visitors: a <a target="_blank" href="https://wise.prf.hn/click/camref:1101l5L5pb/destination:https%3A%2F%2Fwise.com%2Fregister%23%2Femail" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Wise card</a> works like a regular US debit card with no foreign transaction fees and the real exchange rate — no ATM hunting required.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="best-time-to-visit-chicago-for-lower-costs">Best Time to Visit Chicago for Lower Costs</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Shoulder season (April–May, September–October)</strong> is the sweet spot — good weather, lower hotel rates than summer, smaller crowds. September is particularly good: comfortable temperatures, the lake is still swimmable, and summer prices have usually softened.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Winter (November–March)</strong> is usually the cheapest season by a significant margin, but the weather is cold and very windy. Worth it if you do not mind the cold and are primarily interested in indoor attractions.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Summer (June–August)</strong> is peak season — highest prices, most crowded, but the most vibrant atmosphere with beach life, outdoor festivals, and the city at its most active. Book everything months ahead if this is your only option.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Shoulder season tip:</strong> If you visit in September, you're not giving anything up except the peak-summer crowds — the lake is still swimmable, outdoor dining is still running, and the city feels noticeably calmer than July. It's Chicago at a pace that lets you actually enjoy it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="final-thoughts-chicago-travel-costs">Final Thoughts: Chicago Travel Costs</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Chicago is genuinely manageable on a range of budgets — and more so than most comparable US cities. The extraordinary free offering means that even a tight budget delivers a remarkable experience. Add one or two paid attractions per day, eat smart, use the L train, and Chicago travel costs are far more reasonable than the city's size and reputation might suggest.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Plan ahead, book accommodation early, and remember that the best sunset in Chicago — from Milton Lee Olive Park or the Adler Planetarium — costs absolutely nothing.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>How much does a trip to Chicago cost?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A comfortable mid-range trip to Chicago typically costs $180–280 per person per day excluding flights and accommodation, or $350–500 per day all-in with a mid-range hotel. Budget travelers can get by on $80–120 per day excluding accommodation by focusing on Chicago's excellent free attractions and eating at local spots rather than tourist restaurants.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Is Chicago expensive compared to other US cities?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Chicago is significantly cheaper than New York and San Francisco, roughly comparable to Boston, and more expensive than cities like Atlanta or Nashville. The hotel costs are the main driver of overall trip cost — accommodation in Chicago's downtown runs $150–250 per night at mid-range, which is comparable to other major US cities.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>What is the cheapest time to visit Chicago?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>January and February are usually the cheapest months because demand is lower and the weather keeps many visitors away. For a balance of good weather and lower prices, April–May and September–October are the safer shoulder-season picks.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>Is the Chicago CityPASS worth it?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes, if your itinerary includes several major paid attractions. The full Chicago CityPASS is built for five attractions, while Chicago C3 is the shorter three-attraction version. If you only want one or two paid sights, buy individual tickets instead.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></details>
<!-- /wp:details -->

<!-- wp:details -->
<details class="wp-block-details"><summary>How much should I tip in Chicago?</summary><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>18–20% at restaurants is standard and expected — it's non-negotiable in Chicago and across the US. Budget 30–35% on top of menu prices to account for both the 10.25% sales tax and an 18–20% tip. At bars, $1–2 per drink. For guided tours, $5–10 per person.</p>
