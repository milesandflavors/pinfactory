<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Casa Milà and Casa Batlló sit five minutes' walk apart on Passeig de Gràcia, both designed by Antoni Gaudí within a few years of each other, and both currently start around €29 for the basic adult daytime visit. That makes the decision harder, not easier.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>With a full day in Barcelona, doing both is genuinely doable. With one afternoon and a ticket budget that does not stretch to two Gaudí houses, most first-time visitors need to pick one. Here's what actually separates them.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="casa-batllo">Casa Batlló: What to Expect</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Casa Batlló is the dramatic one. The facade — blue and green ceramic scales, bone-shaped balconies, a roofline that reads as a dragon's back — stops foot traffic on Passeig de Gràcia.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Inside, the General Visit ticket includes an augmented-reality audio guide that overlays projected context onto the rooms as you walk through: period furniture, the Batlló family's original interiors, the stained glass as it looked when it was installed. The main living floor has almost no straight lines — the ceiling moves like water, and light filters down through a wall of blue ceramic tile that shifts from deep navy at the top to pale sky-blue at street level.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The rooftop closes out the visit with dragon-scale ceramic tiles, sculptural chimneys, and views over the Eixample grid. If you specifically want the famous warrior-style rooftop chimneys, that is Casa Milà rather than Casa Batlló.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Budget 1 to 1.5 hours. Casa Batlló's official ticket page currently lists the General Visit at about 1 hour 15 minutes, with first access at 9 AM and last access at 7:45 PM. Separate night experiences can run later, but they are different ticket types, so do not plan a late visit without checking the ticket category first.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For the deeper version of this — Casa Batlló alongside the rest of the Block of Discord and the wider Gaudí trail across the city — see the <a href="/casa-batllo-gaudi-trail-barcelona/">full Casa Batlló and Gaudí trail guide</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="casa-mila">Casa Milà (La Pedrera): What to Expect</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":2531,"sizeSlug":"large","linkDestination":"none","align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img src="https://milesandflavors.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/manuel-torres-garcia-EFAGPzy2YmM-unsplash-1024x683.jpg" alt="Casa Milà La Pedrera rooftop chimneys in Barcelona" class="wp-image-2531"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Casa Milà's rooftop — the sculptural warrior-style chimneys that make La Pedrera feel so different from Casa Batlló.</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Casa Milà — universally known as La Pedrera, "the quarry," a nickname locals gave its raw, wave-like stone facade when it was built — is the quieter, stranger sibling. There's no straight wall on the entire building; the limestone facade ripples like a cliffside, and the wrought-iron balconies look like seaweed pulled from the water. Inside, the standard La Pedrera Essential ticket includes the courtyards, the Pedrera Apartment (a recreated bourgeois Barcelona home from the early 1900s, furnished as the building's original residents would have lived), the attic, and the rooftop.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The attic houses <strong>Espai Gaudí</strong> — a genuinely useful stop if you want to understand how his ideas developed across Casa Batlló, Park Güell, and the Sagrada Família, rather than seeing each site in isolation. The rooftop is the reason most people come: sculptural stairwell towers, ventilation towers, and chimneys that look like helmeted guards.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You will often hear them compared with Star Wars stormtroopers, but I would treat that as Barcelona design lore rather than confirmed film history. What matters for your visit is simpler: Casa Milà feels less colorful than Casa Batlló, more sculptural, and usually calmer.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Casa Milà's official ticket page currently separates the daytime Essential visit from the Sunset and Night options. The Essential visit is listed from 9 AM to 5:30 PM, while the Sunset ticket runs from 5:30 PM to 8:30 PM. That makes it important to choose the right ticket type if you are building both Gaudí houses into one day.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="which-to-choose">Which Should You Visit If You Can Only Pick One?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Choose Casa Batlló if:</strong> this is your first trip to Barcelona and you want the single most talked-about Gaudí interior, you're traveling with kids or teenagers who respond better to an immersive, tech-assisted experience than a traditional building tour, or you specifically want the option of a later evening visit.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Choose Casa Milà if:</strong> you're more interested in Gaudí as an architect than in a single showpiece building, you want the Espai Gaudí context before or after visiting Park Güell and the Sagrada Família, or you'd rather have a quieter visit without competing for space around the AR displays.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>If you're still not sure:</strong> Casa Batlló is the safer default for a first-time visitor with limited time — it's the one guidebooks and Instagram feeds are usually referring to, and the immersive format tends to land well even with visitors who don't normally care about architecture.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="doing-both">Doing Both in One Day</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The two buildings are a 5-minute walk apart on Passeig de Gràcia, which makes a back-to-back visit realistic if you have a free afternoon. I would normally do Casa Milà first with a daytime Essential ticket, then walk down to Casa Batlló for a later timed slot.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If the Sunset ticket at Casa Milà fits your date better, reverse the order. Budget 3 to 3.5 hours total for both, including the walk between them and a coffee break, which the Block of Discord has no shortage of.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bundled multi-house tickets can work if the timing and inclusions match your route, but I would compare them carefully before assuming they save money. Casa Milà's official site lists several combined options with other Barcelona or Catalonia sights, while guided combo tours may suit visitors who would rather not manage several timed tickets. The tradeoff is flexibility: once multiple attractions are tied together, changing one time slot can affect the whole day.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="practical-tips">Practical Tips: Tickets, Hours, Crowds</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list {"className":"wp-block-list"} -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Book timed tickets ahead</strong> for both — walk-up availability on peak-season days is unreliable, and both buildings sell out specific entry slots, not just daily capacity.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Casa Batlló's "Be the First" early-entry ticket</strong> (from €45, 8:30 AM access) trades a higher price for meaningfully fewer visitors — worth it in July and August, unnecessary in the shoulder months.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Casa Milà's Sunset ticket</strong> (5:30-8:30 PM) is priced lower than general admission on the official site and avoids the midday crowds, but check the season — daylight hours affect how much you will actually see from the rooftop.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Children:</strong> both buildings currently list free entry for young children — Casa Batlló for ages 0-12, Casa Milà for ages 0-11 — but confirm current age cutoffs and ID rules when booking.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Check the official pages before locking the day:</strong> use <a href="https://www.casabatllo.es/en/online-tickets/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Casa Batlló's ticket page</a> and <a href="https://www.lapedrera.com/en/tickets/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">La Pedrera's ticket page</a> for exact hours, ticket types, and child discounts on your date.</li><!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Combine with the neighborhood:</strong> both sit inside or near the Block of Discord — pair either visit with the <a href="/barcelona-bucket-list/">Barcelona bucket list guide</a> for what else to fit into the same day.</li>

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
