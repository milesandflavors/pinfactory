<p>Rome will surprise you with what it costs — usually in both directions. The iconic sights that feel like they should be free aren't, and the moments you'll remember most (the espresso at a corner bar for €1.30, the trattoria two streets off the tourist strip, the hour you spend watching the light change over the Forum from a viewpoint nobody else has found yet) cost almost nothing.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Colosseum requires a proper ticket. The Vatican requires planning. The Borghese Gallery requires advance planning, especially in high season. And if you eat every meal on the piazzas directly below the tourist landmarks, you'll spend twice what you should for food that's half as good as what's three streets away.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="accommodation">Accommodation Costs in Rome</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Rome has accommodation in every price range, and where you stay matters less for convenience than it does in most cities — almost everything in central Rome is walkable, and the metro fills in the longer gaps. The real difference between neighborhoods is atmosphere, not access.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>What to expect by neighborhood and tier:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Budget (hostel, guesthouse, B&amp;B):</strong> €30–70/night for a private room or dorm — available throughout the city; Termini area has the highest concentration of budget options but is not the most pleasant neighborhood to base yourself in</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Mid-range (3–4 star hotel):</strong> €100–200/night for two — this is where Rome's sweet spot sits; central 4-star hotels with good ratings are very competitive by Western European standards</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Boutique and luxury:</strong> €250–500+/night — Rome has some genuinely extraordinary luxury hotels, particularly around Via Veneto and near the Spanish Steps</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Neighborhood breakdown:</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Trastevere:</strong> €100–200/night for mid-range. The most atmospheric neighborhood in Rome — cobblestones, ivy-covered facades, excellent restaurants. Popular with visitors but not overrun. A good choice if you want Rome to feel cinematic from the moment you step outside.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Monti:</strong> €100–180/night. The neighborhood locals actually like — vintage shops, independent bars, a slightly younger crowd. Close to the Colosseum and the Roman Forum. Walking distance from almost everything.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Vaticano / Prati:</strong> €90–170/night. Quieter and more residential than the historic center, directly north of St. Peter's Square. Makes the Vatican itself a 15-minute walk rather than a metro commute. Good restaurants on Via Cola di Rienzo.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Centro Storico (Pantheon, Campo de' Fiori):</strong> €130–250/night. The absolute center — everything is minutes away. Higher price, lower value per euro than Monti or Trastevere, but the location is extraordinary.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Termini:</strong> €60–120/night. The most budget-friendly area, and well-connected by transit. Not the Rome you're picturing, though — the streets around the central station aren't what most visitors come to experience.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Where I'd stay:</strong> For a first visit to Rome, <a href="https://www.booking.com/hotel/it/michelangelo-hotel.html" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer">Starhotels Michelangelo Rome</a> is the hotel I would shortlist if I wanted a calmer Vatican-side base with an easy walk to St. Peter's instead of paying historic-center prices for a smaller room. Before booking either one, compare the room type, total price, breakfast, and cancellation policy for your exact dates.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="airport-transport">Getting to Rome from the Airport</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Rome has two airports: <strong>Fiumicino (FCO)</strong> is the main international airport, about 30km southwest of the city. <strong>Ciampino (CIA)</strong> is smaller and used mostly by low-cost carriers — closer to the city but less well-connected by train.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>From Fiumicino (FCO):</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Leonardo Express (recommended):</strong> €14/person one way, runs every 30 minutes, 32-minute journey direct to Roma Termini. The fastest and most straightforward option — no stops, no changes, air-conditioned. Buy tickets at the airport kiosks or in advance online.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Regional train FL1:</strong> €8/person, slower (45–60 min), stops at Trastevere, Ostiense, and Tiburtina stations before Termini. Worth it if your hotel is in Trastevere — you arrive directly at the right station.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Taxi:</strong> Fixed official fare of €55 from FCO to central Rome within the Aurelian Walls. Confirm the fixed fare before you get in and use official white taxis only. Not faster than the train, but easier with a lot of luggage and no train changes.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Bus (Terravision, SIT Bus Shuttle):</strong> €6–8/person, 1h+ depending on traffic. The slowest option — skip it unless you're on a very tight budget.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>From Ciampino (CIA):</strong> The Terravision bus to Roma Termini runs every 30–60 minutes and costs €4–6. A taxi is around €40 fixed rate to central Rome. No direct train option — the airport bus is the standard choice here.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="getting-around">Getting Around the City</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Rome's historic center is remarkably walkable — Colosseum to the Pantheon is about 20 minutes on foot, Pantheon to Campo de' Fiori is 5 minutes, Piazza Navona to the Spanish Steps is 15. You'll cover a lot of ground on foot without realizing it, which is also how Rome is supposed to be experienced.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The metro has two main lines (A and B) and a newer line (C). Line A connects the Vatican area (Ottaviano) to the Spanish Steps (Spagna) to the Colosseum via Termini. Line B reaches the Colosseum directly (Colosseo stop). For the Vatican and Spanish Steps, metro A is the practical choice. For the Colosseum and Circo Massimo, metro B.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Single ticket:</strong> €1.50, valid for 100 minutes across metro + bus (one metro journey only per ticket)</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>24-hour pass:</strong> €8.50</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>48-hour pass:</strong> €15</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>72-hour pass:</strong> €22</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>7-day pass:</strong> €29</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For a 3-day visit, the 72-hour pass at €22 makes sense if you plan to use the metro regularly. For shorter stays heavy on walking, single tickets may be enough. Taxis are not expensive for the distances involved — a cross-city ride rarely exceeds €15 — and are the practical choice late at night or in summer heat when the walk genuinely isn't pleasant.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="food-costs">Food and Drink Costs in Rome</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Roman food is some of the best in Europe. It also has a well-earned reputation for tourist traps — and the gap between a good meal at a fair price and an overpriced one for tourists is larger in Rome than almost anywhere else in Italy. The variable is almost always location: walk two streets away from the main piazzas and the prices drop and the quality rises.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Coffee:</strong> The Italian way — standing at the bar, paying when you order, leaving promptly. An espresso is €1–1.50 at the counter. Sitting down at a table bumps this to €3–5. You're not paying for the coffee, you're paying for the seat. For a cornetto (Italian croissant) + espresso: €2.50–4 at the bar, every morning.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Lunch on a budget:</strong> Pizza al taglio (pizza by the cut) is one of the best cheap meals in Rome — a generous slice runs €3–5, and the good spots will let you point at exactly what you want. Supplì (fried rice balls) are €2–3 each. Panino shops, porchetta stalls at Campo de' Fiori, and market food all land in this range. Budget lunch for one: €6–10.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Sit-down trattoria:</strong> Two people, two pastas, a shared antipasto, water, no wine: €35–55 at a genuine neighborhood trattoria. Add wine and it climbs to €50–70. The worst-value meals in Rome happen at restaurants with photographs on the menus facing tourist landmarks — pasta carbonara at €22 in front of the Pantheon versus €14 two streets away, for a meaningfully worse version.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Gelato:</strong> €2.50–4.50 per scoop from a good gelateria. Avoid gelato that comes in huge voluminous piles — authentic gelato is stored flat in metal containers. Quality is consistent across Trastevere and Monti; around the Trevi Fountain and Spanish Steps, you pay more for the same or worse.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

The Trevi Fountain, the Spanish Steps, Campo de' Fiori, the Pantheon exterior, Trastevere's streets, Gianicolo Hill — none of these cost anything. <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="money-saving-tips">Money-Saving Tips for Rome</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Always pay at the bar for coffee.</strong> Sitting down at a café table doubles or triples the price of an espresso, and the coffee is no different. The Italian way — stand at the bar, order, pay, drink, leave — is also faster and more authentic. Once you do it once, you'll wonder why you ever did it any other way.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Walk two streets from any landmark before choosing a restaurant.</strong> The places with menus in six languages and photographs of every dish are consistently the worst value in Rome. The trattoria with a handwritten chalkboard two minutes off the tourist path is where the price drops and the quality rises. This rule alone can save €15–25 per person per meal.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Book the Colosseum and Vatican online in advance.</strong> The online prices are lower than on-site, the time slots are better, and you avoid the queue. In summer the queues at the Vatican gate are legendary for the wrong reasons. There's no advantage to not booking ahead.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>The Pantheon at €5 is one of the best-value paid attractions in the world.</strong> A perfectly preserved 2,000-year-old building in the center of the city, ticketed since 2023 — but still extraordinary value. Do not skip it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Take the Leonardo Express from Fiumicino.</strong> At €14 it's significantly cheaper than a taxi (€55+), 32 minutes direct, and you arrive at Termini with the full metro network available. Only consider the taxi if you have a lot of luggage and are staying somewhere the FL1 regional train doesn't serve.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Buy a transit pass if you're staying three days or more.</strong> The 72-hour pass at €22 pays for itself once you use public transport regularly over three days and removes the mental overhead of managing single tickets. Rome is very walkable, but when you need the metro, you'll use it multiple times per day.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Skip the tourist menus at lunch.</strong> Many trattorias offer a fixed-price lunch (menù del giorno) for €10–15 — a primo (pasta), secondo (meat or fish), and sometimes a dessert. These are often the best value meals in Rome, made from what's fresh that day, and served quickly. Ask if there's a menù del giorno if you don't see it displayed.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="practical-info">Practical Money Info</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Currency:</strong> Euro. Cards are widely accepted in hotels, restaurants, and shops — but Rome has enough small trattorias, market vendors, and older establishments that prefer cash. Keep €30–50 in cash on you for these situations; running out at a market or a neighborhood bar is a real inconvenience.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>ATMs:</strong> Available throughout the city. Use bank ATMs rather than standalone machines in tourist areas, which carry additional fees. The airport exchange desks are expensive — withdraw cash from an in-city ATM instead. For the card side of the trip, a <a href="https://milesandflavors.com/best-travel-money-card/">travel money card</a> avoids foreign transaction fees and bad exchange rates on every purchase abroad.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Tipping:</strong> Not mandatory in Italy. It's common to round up or leave small change at bars and casual restaurants; at a dinner where the service was genuinely good, €2–5 per person is appropriate. The coperto (cover charge, usually €1–3 per person) is a standard line item at sit-down restaurants — it's not a tip and it's not optional, it's just how Italian restaurants work.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Mobile data:</strong> Having data before you land makes everything smoother — navigation works on the metro, you can look up the address of a restaurant without hunting for Wi-Fi. An eSIM installs on your phone before you travel and connects automatically when you land. <a href="https://airalo.pxf.io/3koV4r" target="_blank" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Airalo's Italy eSIM</a> is the one I use — activate it before your flight and it's working the moment you switch airplane mode off at FCO.</p>

<p><strong>Exchange rates and fees:</strong> Rome is a city where small amounts add up — €3 coffee surcharges, €2 coperto, a few extra euro on a taxi. The easy way to avoid paying an extra 3–4% on every card transaction is a fee-free travel card. <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="faq">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="how-much-does-rome-cost-per-day">How much does Rome cost per day?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>On a careful budget: €70–100/person/day including accommodation. For a comfortable mid-range experience with guided tours and sit-down dinners: €130–180/person/day. Accommodation alone in a central 4-star hotel adds €60–100/person/night; food and activities on a mid-range day run €70–90/person on top of that.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="is-rome-expensive">Is Rome expensive?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>By Western European capital standards, Rome sits in the middle — more expensive than Lisbon or Athens, less expensive than Paris or London. The main cost drivers are accommodation (competitive for the quality) and the major attraction tickets (Colosseum around €18, Vatican direct tickets usually around €25 online). Food has an enormous range: you can eat brilliantly for €10–15 at lunch or pay €50+ per person at a landmark restaurant. The difference is usually whether you walk two streets off the tourist trail.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="how-much-spending-money-for-rome">How much spending money do I need for 5 days in Rome?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For two adults for 5 days, budget €1,400–2,000 excluding flights — covering accommodation (€120–200/night × 5), Colosseum and Vatican tours (~€120 for two with guides), daily food (€80–100/day for two at mid-range), airport transport (€56 return for two by Leonardo Express, less if the FL1 works for your station), city transport (€29 for a 7-day pass per person), and some extra for the Borghese Gallery, Pantheon, and gelato. A careful budget with self-guided tickets and cheaper accommodation brings this down to €900–1,100 for two.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="is-the-colosseum-worth-it">Is the Colosseum worth visiting?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yes — and a guided tour is worth the upgrade over a self-guided ticket. The €16 ticket gets you inside; the €49 guided tour (3 hours, includes Roman Forum and Palatine Hill, 24,900+ Viator reviews) tells you what you're actually looking at. The scale of the Colosseum is something photographs genuinely don't communicate until you're standing inside it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="what-is-the-cheapest-way-from-fiumicino">What's the cheapest way from Fiumicino airport to Rome?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The FL1 regional train at €8/person one way — stops at Trastevere, Ostiense, and Tiburtina before Termini, 45–60 minutes. The Leonardo Express at €14/person is faster (32 minutes, direct to Termini) and more convenient with luggage. Both are far cheaper than the official taxi (€55 fixed fare) and faster than the bus. The Leonardo Express is the standard recommendation for most arrivals.</p>
